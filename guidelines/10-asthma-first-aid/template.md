# Asthma attack first aid

About: Guides you through first aid for an asthma attack in New Zealand: sitting the person up, giving reliever puffs, waiting 6 minutes, and calling 111 if they are not improving.
Source: Cardiac and Respiratory Foundation NZ, Asthma First Aid in School poster (July 2026) - https://cardiacandrespiratory.org.nz/documents/72/CRFNZ_Asthma_First_aid_in_school_A3_poster_2026_WWW.pdf
Source: Cardiac and Respiratory Foundation NZ, Asthma First Aid poster, adolescent and adult 12 years + (July 2026) - https://cardiacandrespiratory.org.nz/documents/71/CRFNZ_Asthma_First_aid_poster_12_WWW.pdf
Source: Cardiac and Respiratory Foundation NZ, Asthma web page - https://cardiacandrespiratory.org.nz/your-health/understanding-conditions/asthma/
Source: Health New Zealand Te Whatu Ora, Mate huangō Asthma - https://info.health.nz/conditions-treatments/lungs/asthma
Source: Asthma Australia, Asthma First Aid poster, Blue/Grey Reliever v13 (2026), used only for clearly labelled lines - https://asthma.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/AA26-Blue-1st-Aid-A4_Web-1.pdf
Version: Foundation posters dated July 2026; Foundation and Health NZ web pages undated; Asthma Australia poster v13 (2026); all read 19 Nov 2026
Audience: Anyone helping a child or adult having an asthma attack in New Zealand
Warning: If the person has severe asthma or is frightened, dial 111 for an ambulance now.

## Steps

### start: Check them now
Say: Dial 111 now if they are:
- frightened, or have severe asthma
Quote: "severe asthma or is frightened"
- gasping, or can hardly speak two words
Quote: "gasping for breath, difficulty speaking two words"
- blue lips, or very quiet with no wheeze
Quote: "blueness around their lips"
Quote: "there may be no wheeze"
- breathing fast, or ribs sucking in
Quote: "breathe rapidly"
Quote: "(in-drawing)"
- very tired, floppy or less responsive
Quote: "is very tired or less responsive"
Ask: Which fits?
- Not breathing -> not-breathing
- Known allergy, sudden breathing difficulty -> allergy
- Any sign above: dial 111 -> call-now
- None of these -> sit

### call-now: Dial 111 now
Do: Dial 111 for an ambulance now.
Quote: "dial 111 for an ambulance immediately."
Do: Sit them upright, leaning forward slightly, arms on their knees or a table. Stay with them and reassure them.
Quote: "Sit the child upright and stay with them."
Quote: "Lean them forward slightly"
Quote: "stay with them and reassure them."
Ask: Next?
- Give the puffs -> puffs-6
- Known allergy and sudden breathing difficulty -> allergy
- Not breathing -> not-breathing

### sit: Sit them up
Do: Sit them upright, leaning forward slightly, arms on their knees or a table. Stay with them and reassure them.
Quote: "Sit the child upright and stay with them."
Quote: "Lean them forward slightly"
Quote: "stay with them and reassure them."
Ask: How bad are their symptoms?
- Mild: short of breath, wheeze, cough, tight chest -> puffs-2
- Moderate or worse: loud wheeze, hard to breathe, short sentences only -> puffs-6
- Not sure it is asthma -> not-sure

### puffs-2: Give 2 puffs
Do: Give 2 puffs of reliever, one at a time. 6 slow breaths per puff.
Quote: "2 puffs of reliever inhaler, one puff of medicine at a time, taking 6 breaths per puff."
Quote: "six slow breaths for each puff."
Say: Use a spacer when possible. Help them breathe as normally as possible.
Quote: "using a spacer when possible."
Quote: "breathe as normally as possible."
Say: Have their own action plan? Follow it.
Quote: "or in your action plan."
Ask: Done?
- Done: start the 6-minute wait -> wait
- Aged 12 or over? Read this -> adult-12
- No reliever inhaler here -> no-reliever

### puffs-6: Give 6 puffs
Do: Give 6 puffs of reliever, one at a time. 6 slow breaths per puff.
Quote: "6 puffs of reliever inhaler, one puff of medicine at a time, taking 6 breaths per puff."
Quote: "six slow breaths for each puff."
Say: Use a spacer when possible. Help them breathe as normally as possible.
Quote: "using a spacer when possible."
Quote: "breathe as normally as possible."
Say: Have their own action plan? Follow it.
Quote: "or in your action plan."
Ask: Done?
- Done: start the 6-minute wait -> wait
- Aged 12 or over? Read this -> adult-12
- No reliever inhaler here -> no-reliever

### adult-12: Aged 12 or over
Say: The Foundation's poster for ages 12+ differs:
- One dose of reliever at a time, up to 6 times.
Quote: "Give ONE dose at a time"
Quote: "up to 6 times."
- If not improving, dial 111 at once. No wait time is given.
Quote: "If not improving, dial 111 for an ambulance immediately."
Say: Health NZ uses 6 puffs, 6 breaths, 6 minutes for adults too; this app follows it.
Quote: "take 6 puffs (adults and tamariki)"
Ask: Then?
- Puffs given: start the 6-minute wait -> wait
- Not improving: dial 111 -> call-111

### wait: Wait 6 minutes
Say: Stay with them and watch carefully.
Quote: "watch carefully"
Warning: Call 111 now if they get worse quickly or the reliever isn't working.
Quote: "symptoms get worse very quickly"
Quote: "reliever inhaler has little or no effect"
Quote: "Complaining that the reliever medicine is not working"
Timer: 6 minutes -> check
Quote: "If not improving after 6 minutes"
Ask: Getting worse?
- Worse, or you are frightened: call 111 -> call-111
- Not breathing or not responding -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### check: After 6 minutes
Ask: Are they improving?
- Yes -> monitor
- No: give 6 more puffs and call 111 -> call-111
- Not breathing or not responding -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### call-111: Puffs now, and call 111
Do: Give 6 puffs now, one at a time, as before.
Quote: "repeat the whole sequence of 6 puffs."
Do: Call 111 if not done yet. For a child, contact a parent or caregiver.
Quote: "call an ambulance and contact a parent or caregiver."
Do: Repeat every 6 minutes until help arrives (tap Done on the reminder).
Quote: "every 6 minutes until help arrives"
Remind: every 6 minutes: Give 6 more puffs
Quote: "repeat every 6 minutes."
Say: You will not overdose them.
Quote: "you will not overdose"
Ask: What now?
- Ambulance arrived -> handover
- Not breathing -> not-breathing
- Reliever has run out -> no-reliever

### monitor: They are improving
Do: Stay and keep watching. For a child, contact a parent or caregiver.
Quote: "keep monitoring and contact a parent or caregiver."
Say: Give more puffs if needed. Not getting easier to breathe? Call 111.
Quote: "If necessary, repeat puffs of reliever inhaler."
Quote: "not finding it easier to breathe"
Ask: How are they now?
- No wheeze, cough or breathlessness -> all-ok
- Not getting easier: call 111 -> call-111
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### all-ok: All OK
Say: They can go back to a quiet activity. If symptoms come back, repeat the treatment and rest.
Quote: "return to a quiet activity."
Quote: "If symptoms recur, repeat treatment and rest."
Do: Always see a doctor after an asthma attack.
Quote: "always see a doctor after an asthma attack."
Say: Attacks more frequent or worrying? See a doctor as soon as possible.
Quote: "more frequent or worrying, see your doctor as soon as possible."
Ask: What now?
- Symptoms are back: start again -> start
- Finish -> end

### allergy: Known allergy
Warning: The NZ asthma guidance does not cover this.
Do: Dial 111 now.
Quote: "FOR AN AMBULANCE IMMEDIATELY IF THE PERSON:"
Quote: "Ambulance 111"
Say: Asthma Australia's guidance (not the NZ posters) says: "GIVE ADRENALINE FIRST (if available)". It does not say what next; follow the 111 call taker.
Quote: "SUDDEN BREATHING DIFFICULTY, GIVE ADRENALINE FIRST (if available)"
Ask: Then?
- Adrenaline given, or none here -> sit
- Not breathing -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### not-sure: Not sure it is asthma
Warning: The NZ asthma guidance does not cover this.
Do: Dial 111 now.
Quote: "FOR AN AMBULANCE IMMEDIATELY IF THE PERSON:"
Quote: "Ambulance 111"
Say: Asthma Australia's guidance (not the NZ posters) says to call an ambulance if you are unsure it is asthma.
Quote: "is unsure if it is asthma"
Ask: Then?
- It is asthma -> sit
- Not breathing -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### no-reliever: No reliever inhaler
Warning: The NZ asthma guidance does not say what to do without a reliever.
Do: Dial 111 now. Keep them upright and stay with them.
Quote: "Ambulance 111"
Quote: "Sit the child upright and stay with them."
Say: Asthma Australia's guidance (not the NZ posters) says to call an ambulance at once if no reliever is available.
Quote: "a reliever is not available"
Ask: Then?
- A reliever is here now -> puffs-6
- Not breathing -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### not-breathing: Not breathing
Do: Call 111 now if no one has. Tell the call taker and follow their instructions.
Quote: "is not breathing"
Say: The NZ asthma guidance does not say what to do next.
Note: Gap in the guideline (lessons trap 13). No CPR steps are in these sources, so none are added.
Ask: Then?
- Ambulance arrived -> handover
- Breathing again -> call-111

### handover: The ambulance has arrived
Stop: all
Say: Tell them what happened, how many puffs you gave and when, and follow their advice.
Note: App wording; the sources give no handover step. The log shows when each step was tapped.
Do: Always see a doctor after an asthma attack.
Quote: "always see a doctor after an asthma attack."
End:

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch only (the poster PDFs could not be downloaded), so a quote not found may be the copy's fault. Dropping the PDFs into sources/ would make checking exact. Quotes are kept short so each screen stays near 90 words including its quotes.

Main route (NZ, sources A, C, D): 6 puffs, one at a time, 6 breaths per puff, wait 6 minutes, call 111 if not improving, then 6 puffs every 6 minutes until help arrives. Health NZ (D) gives the same 6-6-6 for "adults and tamariki" when unsure of the dose, so the main route is used for all ages. Mild symptoms get 2 puffs (A, C); moderate or severe get 6 (A, C). D says a mild attack "may only need 1 dose of reliever" and to give the number of puffs the doctor said; the puff screens say "follow their own action plan" (D) instead of D's mild wording. "Slow" breaths and "breathe as normally as possible" are from C.

Repeat timing (lessons trap 6): the 6-minute clock starts when "Done: start the 6-minute wait" is tapped. At minute 6, if not improving, the call-111 screen's first action is "Give 6 puffs now" (D: "after 6 minutes, repeat the whole sequence of 6 puffs"), so the minute-6 dose is not missed. The reminder bar counts 6 minutes from that screen; tapping its Done logs the set and restarts the count. If the person gets worse during the wait (say at minute 2), the "now" set comes early and the next is 6 minutes after it; the sources do not say whether to keep to the original 6-minute rhythm. No Record buttons: the answer buttons and the reminder's Done already log the time, and a second button with the same words was confusing.

Ages 12 and over (source B, same Foundation, July 2026) differs: "Give ONE dose at a time", "Repeat if necessary, up to 6 times", then "If not improving, dial 111 for an ambulance immediately" with no wait time, and the reliever "every few minutes". Its reliever examples include anti-inflammatory relievers (Symbicort, Vannair, DuoResp Spiromax) where one dose means something different. A second route with no timings could not be timed without inventing a number, so B is a separate screen (from "Aged 12 or over? Read this" on the puff screens) that says plainly what differs, that Health NZ uses 6-6-6 for adults too, and that the app follows Health NZ; it offers "Not improving: dial 111" straight away, as B says. Lessons trap 5: shown, not hidden; flagged here for the reviewer. B's "Be calm and reassuring" is no longer used; the reassure line quotes D.

Calling 111: A says call an ambulance if not improving after 6 minutes, and dial 111 at once if severe or frightened. D is weaker ("If there is no improvement in symptoms, see a doctor immediately", "In an emergency call 111"). The app follows A (the more specific instruction). Start-screen signs: A's severe list and "has severe asthma or is frightened", plus D's severe signs (blue lips, quiet with no wheeze, rapid breathing, in-drawing) and D's "Call 111 if your child" list (very tired, less responsive, floppy, not breathing). That D list is for children; the app shows it for all ages. C's "symptoms get worse very quickly", "reliever inhaler has little or no effect" and "Complaining that the reliever medicine is not working" are on the wait screen.

Australian guidance (source E, 4 puffs / 4 breaths / 4 minutes / 000) is never used for numbers. It is shown only where the NZ sources are silent, labelled "Asthma Australia's guidance (not the NZ posters)": no reliever available, not sure it is asthma, and known allergy with sudden breathing difficulty ("GIVE ADRENALINE FIRST (if available)"). Each of these screens says "Dial 111 now" (E: "FOR AN AMBULANCE IMMEDIATELY", with NZ's 111 from poster A in place of 000). The allergy question is on the start screen above the severe-signs answer and again on the call-now screen, so a person with severe signs still sees it. No adrenaline dose is given anywhere in these sources, and the app gives none; E does not say what to do after adrenaline, so the screen says to follow the 111 call taker and then offers to carry on with asthma first aid. "Not sure it is asthma" is on the sit screen.

Gaps shown on screen (lessons trap 13): what to do with no reliever, not sure it is asthma, known allergy (NZ silent), and what to do if the person is not breathing (no CPR in these sources; the app says to follow the 111 call taker). Gaps noted here only: how to give puffs without a spacer (NZ says "using a spacer when possible" but not how without one; only E says, so it is left out); whether to stop the 6-minute puffs if the person recovers before the ambulance arrives (A says continue "until help arrives", so the reminder runs until handover); the "If necessary, repeat puffs" line in Monitor gives no number or interval.
Left out on purpose: D's "If you are having the attack, get someone to stay with you" (the app is for helpers; see Audience).
