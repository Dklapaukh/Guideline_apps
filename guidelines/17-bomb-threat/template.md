# Bomb threat

About: Helps the person who takes a bomb threat by phone in New Zealand: what to ask and write down during the call, who to tell, and what to write after it. Also covers a threat by email or social media, and a suspicious item someone finds. It keeps the times Police will want.
Source: NZ Police, "Bomb threat checklist" card (POL 940, printed 11/02) (main source) - https://www.police.govt.nz/sites/default/files/publications/bomb-threat-checklist.pdf
Source: NZ Police, web page "Suspicious mail and bomb threats" - https://www.police.govt.nz/advice-services/businesses-and-organisations/suspicious-mail-and-bomb-threats
Source: NZ Police, web page "How you can help" (Protecting crowded places from attack) - https://www.police.govt.nz/advice-services/protecting-crowded-places-attack/how-you-can-help
Source: ANZCTC, "Improvised Explosive Device (IED) Guidelines for Crowded Places" (Commonwealth of Australia 2023), Appendix B - https://www.nationalsecurity.gov.au/crowded-places-subsite/Files/ied-guidelines-crowded-places.pdf
Version: NZ Police 'Bomb threat checklist' card (POL 940, printed '11/02', no other date; no newer NZ version found); NZ Police web pages 'Suspicious mail and bomb threats' and 'How you can help' (no dates shown, footer 2026); ANZCTC 'Improvised Explosive Device (IED) Guidelines for Crowded Places' (Commonwealth of Australia 2023, Appendix B Telephoned bomb threat checklist); all read 20 Nov 2026
Audience: Anyone in a New Zealand workplace who answers the phone, and the colleague helping them
Warning: In New Zealand call 111. Best used by a colleague beside the person on the phone. The NZ card is old (11/02); the Australian checklist is shown where it adds or differs, and says 000.

## Steps

### start: Bomb threat: what is happening?
Say: Type what the caller says into the boxes as you go; this app keeps the times. The paper NZ Police checklist card is the official form: keep it by the phone too.
Quote: "Place this card under your telephone" (NZ Police card)
Quote: "print it out and keep it by your phone." (NZ Police web)
Ask: What is happening?
- A caller is making a threat now -> call
- The threat came by email or social media -> online
- Someone found a suspicious item or bag -> item
- The threat call has ended -> ended

### call: On the phone now: stay calm
Record: Call started
Do: Stay calm. Talk to the caller.
Quote: "Remain calm and talk to the caller" (ANZCTC)

Quote: "Note the caller's number if displayed on your phone" (ANZCTC)
Quote: "If you are able to, record the call" (ANZCTC)

### words: Write the exact words

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch only (police.govt.nz, protectivesecurity.govt.nz and nationalsecurity.gov.au all blocked direct download; see meta.json), so a quote not found may be the copy's fault. Lines in [square brackets] in source.txt are notes, never quoted.

Which source leads: the NZ Police card (A) is the main source; NZ Police web pages (B) add who decides (employer) and the unattended-bag steps; the ANZCTC IED Guidelines (C) are a joint Australia-NZ committee document but an Australian publication (dial 000), shown second and labelled "Australian" wherever used. Differences kept visible: (1) Question order: NZ card starts "When" then "Where"; ANZCTC starts "Where" then "When". The app asks the NZ card's 10 questions in its order, then ANZCTC's extra ones (contains, detonated, who placed it, phone number, group or alone) on q-extra, which also says ANZCTC asks "where" first (moved off q1 so the most urgent screen holds one question). ANZCTC's "Why have you placed the bomb?", name and address overlap with NZ 7, 8, 10 and are not repeated. (2) Emergency number: 111 everywhere, quoting NZ web ("In an emergency everyone should call 111", "call 111 and report it."). The card's "Trace 111" is a field with a blank, not an instruction: it is shown as its own field with a box on tell, never as the quote for calling 111. ANZCTC's "DIAL 000" / "Dial 000" / "Triple Zero" lines are shown only as Australian, never as saying 111; "Call Triple Zero '000' and advise security;" supports "advise security" on item-au, with a line saying the NZ equivalent is the 111 call already made. (3) Who to tell: NZ card "Report call immediately to:" a blank to fill in locally, then Police; ANZCTC building security or coordinating manager, then police. Both on tell. (4) Voice, language and background lists differ (NZ free-text fields; ANZCTC tick boxes): NZ shown in full; ANZCTC extras summarised. (5) Only ANZCTC gives during-call instructions (remain calm, talk to the caller, note the number, record the call); shown on call.

Card order: the details screens follow the card's sections A4 (voice), A5 and A6 with the kind of call printed beside A6 (sounds), A7, A8 number called, A9 (call-info). Two exceptions, on purpose: the exact wording (A2) is asked before the questions (A1), as ANZCTC does, because the words come first in the call; and A8 date, time and duration are on ended (with a box), so they are captured on every phone route, including "The threat call has ended" from start, where "Call started" was never tapped. The app does not work out the call length; the screen says the Log has both times when both were tapped.

Gaps shown on screen (trap 13): the NZ card gives no during- or after-call instructions, and no source says "don't hang up" or to keep the line open, nor how to keep the caller talking beyond "talk to the caller" (ended); no plain rule for when to evacuate after a phone threat: NZ leaves it to the employer, ANZCTC to the incident/security manager with Police, and the app never decides (decide, trap 8); ANZCTC's "Generally speaking, all threats should be deemed credible" is shown with its hedge; the ANZCTC chart linking its 4 assessment questions to its 3 responses was not captured, so they are listed, not linked; no evacuation distance for a phone threat with no item found (decide, evacuate, evacuate-part); what to do when a search finds nothing (search-none); the 25 m / 100 m / 500 m figures apply only to a found suspicious item and are Australian; NZ sources give no distances or radio/mobile rule, and do not say where to call 111 from, so the Australian 25 m warning is shown on item before the 111 call (item-noowner), without inventing a distance to call from; the NZ unattended-bag steps apply only to a bag "with no apparent reason for it being there", and "keep away" only "if there's no obvious owner" (trap 10): item asks this, and item-owned says the guides give no next step; ANZCTC's actions apply "if the item is suspicious" after its three questions (hidden, looks like an explosive device or hazardous material, out of place), asked on item-noowner; if all three are no, item-not says the guides do not say and Police (already called) direct; "Trace 111" is not explained on the card (ask Police); "Members name" under "Police Advised" is not explained either: the screen says "perhaps the Police member you spoke to" and the box is labelled "Members name (card)"; the web-log time span (7 days before to 48 hours after, from a summary only) is not shown as its exact words were not captured; search procedure and assembly points are left to local plans; no handover list in either source (handover says the list is the app's summary). The NZ card is dated 11/02 (November 2002 or February 2011) and no newer NZ version was found: said in the Warning banner. The banner's "Best used by a colleague beside the person on the phone" is the app's own design advice, not a source instruction.

Design: the person on the phone has it at their ear, so the app is meant for a colleague or a second screen. While the caller is on the line each screen holds one or two things: call has one Record ("Call started") and two short ticks; words has one box; each question has its answer box, a "Qn no answer or refused" Record and two answers (Next question / Caller hung up), so a hang-up at any point jumps to ended. Write boxes (text goes to the Log with times and to the Finished screen, only on this phone): caller's exact words; answer boxes on q1-q10 and q-extra; date, time and length of call (ended); person told, Trace 111, Members name (tell); voice, recognised voice (voice); threat language, background noises, kind of call (sounds); sex and age, number called, your name and phone (call-info); who decided (stay); sender address or username (online); what the item looks like and where (item-noowner). The paper card is still named as the official form. Record buttons log key times: Call started, Qn no answer or refused, Call ended, Manager or security told, Police called (111), Evacuation / Partial evacuation / Search started, Search finished: nothing found, Threat message seen, Suspicious item found, Area cleared. The decision itself is logged by the answer on decide (no duplicate Record). Caller details can be reached from tell, waiting and handover, so choosing the decision first never loses them. No timers or reminders: no source gives a time. C5's secondary-explosions warning is shown (Australian) on evacuate and evacuate-part next to the assembly-point warning; C3 "create a plan" (preparation) and C6 stand-off (site design) are left out. ANZCTC's "remarks / additional notes" and signature-block labels were not captured and are not quoted.
