Bomb threat by phone (what to ask and write down during the call, who to tell, search and evacuate-or-not),
word-for-word copies from two official sources (read 20 Nov 2026, NZ time). The sources do NOT say the same
things (for example the NZ Police card gives 10 questions starting "When is the Bomb going to explode?";
the ANZCTC checklist gives 11 questions starting "Where exactly is the bomb right now?"; NZ Police phone
number is 111, the Australian document says 000), so each is kept under its own heading; never mix them.
Main source is SOURCE A.
Capture method (web-fetch copy): direct download is refused here (the network proxy blocks police.govt.nz,
protectivesecurity.govt.nz and nationalsecurity.gov.au) and web search gave titles only, so every line was
read by a fetch tool asked to quote exact lines (long sentences in pieces, rejoined here). Small slips
(apostrophe, capital letter or dash style) are possible. Lines in [square brackets] are notes added here, not
guideline text. On the forms, "...." = a blank line to write on, "[ ]" = a tick box. "-" = bullet.

It can be helpful to have a checklist for recording information if a bomb threat is received.

B2. NZ Police page "How you can help" (Protecting crowded places from attack; no date shown, footer 2026)
https://www.police.govt.nz/advice-services/protecting-crowded-places-attack/how-you-can-help
Unattended or someone concealing bags
Unattended bags or packages may contain dangerous contents.
If you see an unattended package or bag in a public place, with no apparent reason for it being there:
- don't touch it.
- ask people nearby if anyone owns it.
- if there's no obvious owner, tell other people to keep away.
- call 111 and report it.
Report suspicious behaviour
In an emergency everyone should call 111

C4. Assessing unattended and suspicious items (page 12)
That is, anything that is Hidden, Obviously suspicious or not Typical to its environment could be deemed a
security risk.
Is the item intentionally hidden?
Does the item have characteristics of an explosive device or hazardous material, [rest not captured]
Is the item out of place for the location?
[If the item is suspicious:]
- Do not touch, tilt or tamper;
- Do not use mobile phones or radios within a 25 metre radius;
- Call Triple Zero '000' and advise security;
- Cordon off immediate area and evacuate at least 100 metres from the item (if a vehicle, evacuate at least
  500 metres);
- Ensure no personnel are within line of sight of the suspicious item;
- Record all relevant information (physical characteristics, shape, dimensions, construction and the exact
  position of the item or vehicle);
- Respond in accordance with the directions of emergency services; and
- Gather any evidence of the placement of the item or vehicle, including CCTV showing activities of any
  person associated with the item or vehicle.

C5. Evacuation (pages 12-13)
Planning and initiating evacuation should be the responsibility of the incident/security manager and, if
possible, made in consultation with police and other emergency services.
Depending on the size and nature of the crowded place, the plan may include considerations for total
evacuation or partial evacuation, for example, if the suspicious item is small and thought to be confined to
one location.
The decision to initiate a total or partial evacuation may be determined by several factors
- the nature of the threat
- the potential to cause panic
- potential business impacts from total evacuation
[Evacuation routes and assembly points:] including consideration of secondary devices at assembly points and
procedures to ensure these are kept clear
Be aware that secondary explosions may occur at or near the original blast site, especially as rescue
personnel arrive.

C6. Stand-off (page 5)
Stand-off distance between an IED and crowded areas is the most important factor [rest not captured]
Every metre of stand-off counts in mitigating the effects of a blast.
