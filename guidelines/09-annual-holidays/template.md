# Annual holidays (NZ)

About: Helps employees and employers in New Zealand with annual holidays: asking for time off, when an employer can make you take holidays (including closedowns), cashing up, cancelling, and holiday pay. It follows the current rules in the Holidays Act; Employment New Zealand says the Employment Leave Act will replace it in August 2028.

## Steps

### start: What do you need?
Say: After 12 months with your employer, you are entitled to at least 4 weeks of paid annual holidays.
Quote: "You are entitled to at least 4 weeks of paid annual holidays (annual leave) when you have worked for your employer for 12 months."
Ask: What do you need help with?
- I want to ask for time off -> entitled
- My employer wants me to take holidays, or there's a closedown -> required
- I want to cash up some holidays (get paid instead) -> cashup
- I'm an employer -> employer
- Something else (cancelling, holiday pay, leaving a job) -> other

### other: Something else
Ask: What do you want to know about?
- Cancelling holidays that are already arranged -> cancel
- How much is "a week" of holidays for me? -> week
- How holiday pay is worked out -> holiday-pay
- Holidays when someone leaves a job -> leaving

### entitled: Are you entitled yet?
Say: You get at least 4 weeks after each 12 months of continuous work with your employer.
Quote: "After each 12 months of continuous employment with an employer, you are entitled to no less than 4 weeks of annual holidays as paid time off work."
Say: Your employer cannot give you less than 4 weeks a year, even if your employment agreement says so.
Quote: "You cannot give an employee less than 4 weeks annual holidays each year, even if their employment agreement says so."
Say: Holidays you are entitled to don't expire.
Quote: "Your annual holidays entitlement stays available until you have taken the holidays or it has been paid out ('cashed-up')."
Ask: Which is true for you?
- I've worked 12 months or more and have holidays left -> ask-time
- I haven't worked 12 months yet, or I've used all my holidays -> advance
- I'm paid holiday pay with my wages (casual or short fixed-term) -> payg

### payg: Holiday pay with your wages
Say: Some employees can agree to have holiday pay paid with their regular pay instead of taking time off. This is called "pay-as-you-go".
Quote: "In some cases, you can agree with your employer to have your annual holidays paid out with your regular pay instead of taking time off."
Say: It can only happen if you are on a fixed-term agreement of less than 12 months, or your work is so irregular that 4 weeks' holidays in the normal way is impossible in practice.
Quote: "are on a fixed-term agreement of less than 12 months, or"
Quote: "work so irregularly or intermittently that it is impossible in practice for your employer to provide you with 4 weeks' annual holidays in the normal way."
Say: The pay is at least 8% of gross earnings on top of your usual pay.
Quote: "Certain employees can agree to be paid at least 8% of gross earnings in addition to their usual pay instead of taking annual holidays, but only if they meet certain criteria."

### closedown: Annual closedown
Say: An annual closedown is when a workplace (or part of it) shuts each year and staff must take time off.
Quote: "An annual closedown is when a workplace shuts each year, often for a holiday or seasonal break, and employees are required to take time off."
Say: An employer can have only 1 annual closedown in any 12-month period, with 14 days' notice before it starts.
Quote: "An employer can only have 1 annual closedown in any 12-month period and must give their employees 14 days' notice before it starts."

- Some, but not enough -> closedown-short
