# Power Cut: Keep or Throw Out Food

About: Helps you look after the food in your fridge and freezer during a power cut, and decide what to keep, refreeze or throw out once you check it. It shows what each New Zealand source says, side by side where they disagree.

## Steps

### start: Power cut: what to do now
Do: Keep the fridge and freezer doors shut. Open them as little as you can.
Quote: "During the power failure, open your fridge or freezer as little as possible to help keep it cooler for longer." (MPI fact sheet)
Quote: "The first advice is to not open the door of the refrigerator or freezer." (Northland)
Do: Tell everyone in the house, including the children. A big notice on the door can help.
Quote: "Make sure you tell everyone in the house including the children. It might be a good idea to hang a big notice on the door to remind everyone." (Northland)
Do: Eat from the fridge first, then the freezer, then the pantry or your emergency kit.
Quote: "EAT FROM THE FRIDGE FIRST, THE FREEZER SECOND, AND THE PANTRY LAST." (QLDC)
Quote: "If the power goes out, eat the food from your fridge first, then your freezer. Then eat the food in the cupboard or your emergency kit." (Get Ready)
Do: Eat foods that will expire soon first, such as bread, meat, salads and dairy products.
Quote: "Eat foods that will expire soon first – such as bread, meat, salads, and dairy products – because they spoil more quickly than non-perishable food." (MPI fact sheet)
Do: Leave frozen food in the freezer if you can.
Quote: "So, leave already frozen food in the freezer if possible." (MPI fact sheet)
Ask: Is the power still off?
- Still off: it has just gone off -> just-off
- Still off: it has been off for a while -> during
- It is back on -> back-on

### fridge-between: Fridge food: 2 to 4 hours
Say: MPI, NEMA, the QLDC poster and FSANZ say an unopened fridge holds its cold for about 4 hours.
Quote: "Unopened fridges should hold refrigeration temperature for 4 hours." (MPI fact sheet)
Quote: "A fridge should keep food cold for around four hours - after that it can begin to spoil." (FSANZ)
Say: Northland Regional Council says "at least 2 hours". That is a minimum, not a limit, so it does not say the food is warm after 2 hours.
Quote: "An unopened refrigerator will keep your food cool for at least 2 hours." (Northland)
Say: Northland: food at 5 degrees Celsius or above for more than 2 hours is much more likely to grow germs that cause illness.
Quote: "Once food reaches the temperature of 5 degrees Celsius or above for more than 2 hours it greatly increases the risk of growth by microorganisms that can cause illness." (Northland)
Do: Check each food. Does it smell or look different? Has the colour changed, or is it slimy? If so, bin it.
Quote: "Does the food from your fridge, freezer or pantry smell or look different? Has the colour changed and does it have a slimy texture? If so, it's likely unsafe and should be binned." (MPI fact sheet)
Warning: Don't taste food to see if it's OK. If in doubt, throw it out.

### fridge-warm: Fridge food may have warmed up
Say: If you're not sure how long it has been, treat the food as warmed up.
Quote: "If in doubt, throw it out." (MPI fact sheet)
Warning: Northland: don't use fridge food that has warmed up. The only exceptions are butter or margarine, hard cheeses, raw eggs still in their shells, fruit and vegetables.
Quote: "Do not use frozen food that has thawed or refrigerated food that has warmed up. The only exceptions to this are butter/margarine, hard cheeses, raw eggs (still in eggshells), fruit and vegetables." (Northland)
Say: Auckland Regional Public Health Service (in a news item during a long power cut): "after today", meat and fish from the fridge may not be safe. It gives no number of hours. Raw vegetables, cheese and bread can still be eaten (it says "cheese", not only hard cheese).
Quote: "After today, highly perishable foods in the fridge like meat and fish may not be safe to eat," (ARPHS)
Quote: "You can still eat food like raw vegetables, cheese and bread." (ARPHS)
Do: Check every food you keep. Does it smell or look different? Has the colour changed, or is it slimy? If so, bin it.
Quote: "Does the food from your fridge, freezer or pantry smell or look different? Has the colour changed and does it have a slimy texture? If so, it's likely unsafe and should be binned." (MPI fact sheet)

Quote: "Don't taste it to see if it's OK." (FSANZ)

### thawed: Thawed: don't refreeze it
Warning: Don't refreeze food that has thawed. (Northland's table makes one exception, for fruit: see "Fruit or vegetables".)
Quote: "If the food has thawed, you should not refreeze it." (MPI fact sheet)
Quote: "When the power comes back on, it is important not to refreeze food that has defrosted." (ARPHS)
Quote: "If frozen foods have thawed don't refreeze!" (FSANZ)
Say: Keep it or throw it out? The sources disagree:
- MPI and NEMA: you can still keep or use thawed food if it has not started to look or smell bad. Keep it cold, like in the fridge, until you eat or cook it.
Quote: "You can still keep or use thawed food if it has not started to look or smell bad. Keep it cold (like in the fridge) until you are ready to eat or cook it." (MPI fact sheet)
Quote: "You can still keep or use thawed food if it has not started to look or smell bad. Keep it cold (like in the fridge) until you eat or cook it." (NEMA)

## Notes

Sources and their limits
- Eight sources (A to H in source.txt), all read with a fetch tool (sourceCopy web-fetch); D, G and H were read once only. Small wording slips are possible.
- The MPI fact sheet was written for floods (Cyclone Gabrielle, Feb 2023), not power cuts alone. MPI has no stand-alone power-cut page that could be found. Only its power-failure, refreezing and disposing parts are used, plus the floodwater line (shown only as "If there was flooding").
- Northland Regional Council names no source organisation; its frozen-food table (10°C / 8 hours rows) looks adapted from overseas, but this is not stated. It is shown as "Northland's table" and every line says which organisation says it.
- FSANZ is the joint Australia-New Zealand agency; its eskies line is labelled "Australia and New Zealand".
- The MPI fact sheet line "and cause of food poisoning" is a typo in the source; it is not quoted.
- One MPI web page sentence is missing its start in the saved copy ("[...] fridges, freezers, and ovens may break down"); not used.

Contradictions (shown side by side on screen, never silently picked)
1. Fridge hold time: MPI, NEMA and QLDC say an unopened fridge holds refrigeration temperature for 4 hours; FSANZ says around four hours; Northland says at least 2 hours. Shown on "While the power is off" and on the fridge screens; the fridge question splits at 2 and 4 hours so each source's time is a boundary, and the 2-4 hour answer goes to a screen that shows both and explains that Northland's "at least 2 hours" is a minimum, not a limit (so it is not a true disagreement).
2. Freezer hold time: MPI/NEMA/QLDC full 2-4 days, half full 1-2 days; Northland half full up to 24 hours, full 48 hours; FSANZ around 24 hours with no split. Shown in the "Freezer hold time" table (one column per source), keyed by the "Freezer" detail (Full / Half full / Not sure how full).
3. Thawed food: MPI and NEMA say thawed food can be kept or used if it does not look or smell bad and is kept cold; Northland says do not use thawed frozen food (except butter/margarine, hard cheeses, raw eggs in shell, fruit and vegetables); ARPHS says dispose of freezer food thawed and at room temperature for longer than two hours. All three are shown on "Thawed: don't refreeze it" and a short MPI reminder sits on each thawed food screen.
4. Ice cream and frozen yoghurt: Northland says throw out even with ice crystals; MPI's general rule says visibly frozen food can be refrozen. Flagged as a warning on the refreeze screen.
5. Thawed fruit: Northland's table says refreeze (throw out if mould, yeasty smell or sliminess develops); MPI, NEMA, ARPHS and FSANZ say don't refreeze thawed food. Flagged on the fruit screen.
6. Cheese: Northland's exceptions say "hard cheeses"; ARPHS says "cheese". Bread: ARPHS says bread can still be eaten; Northland's exceptions list does not include bread. Both shown on the fridge-warm screen.
7. Time limit for warm food: Northland 5 degrees Celsius / 2 hours (and 10°C / 8 hours for bread, plain cakes and vegetables); ARPHS two hours at room temperature; MPI no time, only look and smell. ARPHS's "After today" for fridge meat and fish (said in a news item during a multi-day outage) gives no hours and no start point; shown in its own words, "after today", and the screen says it gives no number of hours.
8. Vegetables: Northland contradicts itself. Its exceptions list says thawed fruit and vegetables can still be used; its table says thawed vegetables are thrown out if above 10°C for over 8 hours. Flagged as a warning on the fruit and vegetables screen.

Gaps (said on screen where they matter)
- No source defines "full" or "half full", or covers a nearly empty freezer, chest versus upright freezers, or opening the door (beyond "the more times you open the door, the quicker things will go off"). The "Not sure how full" row shows both of each source's figures (half full and full) and says the half full time is the shorter one, without choosing a new number; a line says no source defines full or half full.
- No source says how to tell how long the power was off or how warm food got (no clock, thermometer or ice-cube tips), or what to do if you were away. The fridge screen says so; "Not sure" on the fridge question goes to the warmed-up screen, applying "If in doubt, throw it out" (quoted).
- Only Northland gives temperatures (5°C, and 10°C in its table). No source mentions 4°C or a fridge thermometer.
- Frozen food in damaged or open packaging: MPI and ARPHS only allow refreezing with intact packaging; no source says what to do otherwise. The refreeze screen says so and falls back on "If in doubt, throw it out". Northland's table does not mention packaging.
- Northland's thawed column for bread, plain cakes and vegetables only covers "above 10°C for over 8 hours"; nothing says what happens below that or whether they can be refrozen. Said on those screens.
- No food-by-food list for fridge food beyond Northland's exceptions and ARPHS's raw vegetables, cheese and bread. Nothing on leftovers, cooked rice, sliced meats, sauces, baby formula, breast milk or medicines such as insulin; the fridge-warm screen says so and gives the ARPHS Healthline line (the only health contact in the sources). No advice is invented for them.
- Northland's table has no row for other frozen foods; "Something else" goes to a screen with the general look-and-smell rule and Northland's exceptions (butter/margarine, hard cheeses, raw eggs in shell).
- Only FSANZ mentions eskies with ice bricks or gel packs; no NZ source mentions chilly bins, ice or moving food elsewhere.

Design decisions
- Level 3, "right information at the right time": the first screen gives what to do now (doors shut, tell the household, eating order, leave frozen food in the freezer), then one question at a time.
- Power still off: "While the power is off" (fridge and freezer times, check the label, eskies as a "before" tip), then a choice: fridge, freezer, tins/cooking/hands, "The power has come back on", or "Nothing to check right now". That last one goes to "Power still off: come back later" (a waiting screen), never to the end, so the person can come back when the power returns without "Start again" (which would wipe the recorded time and the reminders). The waiting screen offers the power-back-on route, the fridge, the freezer, tins and hygiene, and Finish. The hygiene screen and every thawed food screen also offer "Power is still off", which goes back to the waiting screen.
- Freezer while the power is still off: "Freezer food while the power is off" (eat fridge first then freezer, keep it shut, the hold-time table, ARPHS's two-hour room-temperature rule, FSANZ's "don't refreeze") and, for a food that has thawed, the thawed screens. It never offers refreezing while the power is off; the ice crystal / refreeze question is only on the power-back-on route.
- Power back on: the freezer first (MPI: "Once the power is back on, check the food in your freezer"), one food at a time ("Power is back on: check another frozen food"), then "I've checked all the frozen food" moves on to the fridge, pantry and hygiene screens. Every food screen also offers Finish.
- One detail only, "Freezer" (Full / Half full / Not sure how full), asked on the start screen because it picks the freezer hold-time row. The type of frozen food is asked as a question on the screen where it is needed (four groups following Northland's own table groups, plus "Something else"), not as a detail: the runtime only asks details on the start screen or the About tab, and a 12-button food list before the person has even opened the freezer would be too many decisions at once. Each food screen lists Northland's rows for that group with the row quoted from the table.
- The ice crystal question follows MPI's "still visibly frozen (for example, ... ice crystals)". "No, or I'm not sure" goes to the thawed screen, because only visibly frozen food may be refrozen.
- Packaging is a warning on the refreeze screen, not a separate question, to keep the number of taps down.
- The "Note when the power went off" screen is only offered when the power has just gone off. It has a Record button (time stamp in the Log) and four one-off reminders, each starting "N hours since the power went off" so it reads as time passed, not time left: 2 hours (Northland fridge "at least 2 hours"), 4 hours (MPI/NEMA/QLDC "4 hours"; FSANZ "around four hours"), 24 hours (FSANZ freezer; Northland half full) and 48 hours (Northland full freezer). They don't depend on the Freezer detail: the sources' freezer times disagree, so each source's single time is reminded and named. The MPI day ranges for freezers are not reminded (they are ranges, not one time); they are in the table. "The power is back on" stops all reminders. No timers: the person never waits through a time on one screen.
- The start-screen Warning ("If in doubt, throw it out. Don't taste food") repeats quoted lines from MPI, Northland, QLDC and FSANZ.
- Left out as not about food in a power cut: NEMA torches line, Get Ready "Stock up" (preparing beforehand), hygiene parts of the flood fact sheet, the salmonella/campylobacter background line (ARPHS), and Northland's introduction.
- The freezer hold-time table is shown on "While the power is off" and "Freezer food while the power is off", where it matters (deciding how long to keep the freezer shut). After the power is back on, the ice crystal question decides.
- "Eat foods that will expire soon first" is not limited to the fridge on screen (MPI and NEMA don't say "in the fridge"; only the QLDC poster's box layout suggests it).
- The thawed meat answer names Northland's own row ("casseroles, stews, soups, pizza, sausage rolls") rather than a wider group like "ready meals".
- testInputs in meta.json run the explorer once per Freezer choice (Full, Half full, Not sure how full).
- The detail is labelled just "Freezer" because the reader splits a condition at the word "is", so a label like "How full is your freezer" can't be used in "[if ...]".
