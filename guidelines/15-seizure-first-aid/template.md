# Seizure first aid

About: Guides you through first aid while someone in New Zealand has a seizure (fit or convulsion): keeping them safe, timing it, when to call 111, the recovery position, and what to tell the ambulance. Includes a child with a fever.
Source: Epilepsy New Zealand, poster "Adult first aid for seizures" (main source) - https://epilepsy.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Adult-first-aid-for-seizures-2020-.pdf
Source: Epilepsy New Zealand, web page "Seizure First Aid" - https://epilepsy.org.nz/Seizure-First-Aid
Source: Epilepsy New Zealand, web page "Tonic-clonic seizures" - https://epilepsy.org.nz/understanding-epilepsy/seizure-first-aid/tonic-clonic-seizures/
Source: Hato Hone St John NZ, "Seizures or convulsions" - https://www.stjohn.org.nz/first-aid/first-aid-library/seizures-or-convulsions
Source: ANZCOR Guideline 9.2.4, First Aid Management of a Seizure (June 2024) - https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/9-2-4-first-aid-management-of-a-seizure
Source: KidsHealth NZ, "Febrile seizures in children" (reviewed 6 June 2024) - https://www.kidshealth.org.nz/febrile-seizures-in-children
Version: Epilepsy New Zealand poster 'Adult first aid for seizures' (2020 in the file name, no date printed) and web pages 'Seizure First Aid' and 'Tonic-clonic seizures' (no date shown, footer Copyright 2026); Hato Hone St John 'Seizures or convulsions' first aid library page (no date shown, footer 2025); ANZCOR Guideline 9.2.4 'First Aid Management of a Seizure' (Approved June 2024); KidsHealth NZ 'Febrile seizures in children' (Last reviewed 6 June 2024); all read 20 Nov 2026
Audience: Anyone helping a person having a seizure in New Zealand
Warning: If in doubt, call 111. The sources do not all agree; where they differ, the app shows each one.

## Steps

### start: Seizure now: keep them safe
Remind: after 5 minutes: Seizure 5 minutes: still going? Call 111
Quote: "Seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Say: The 5-minute bar counts from opening this screen. Add any time before that.
Quote: "Time the seizure." (ANZCOR)
Do: Stay calm and with them. Ease their fall if you can. Move hard things away; cushion the head.
Quote: "Stay calm and remain with the person" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "If possible, ease the person's fall." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "move thing out of the way and cushion the head" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Do: Watch their breathing. Food, fluid or vomit in the mouth? Roll them on their side now.
Quote: "Observe and monitor breathing" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "If they have food, fluid or vomit in their mouth roll them on to their side immediately" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Warning: Do not hold them down or move them (unless in danger). Nothing in their mouth.
Quote: "DO NOT move the person, or try to restrain them." (St John)
Quote: "Move person unless in danger" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "DO NOT put anything in the person's mouth" (St John)
Ask: What now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- In water -> water
- Call 111? See the list -> call-list
- Child with a fever -> fever
- More: their side, plan, other -> during
- It has stopped -> after

### during: While it lasts
Say: When to roll them onto their side. The sources differ:
- Epilepsy NZ poster: after jerking stops, or at once if food, fluid or vomit is in the mouth.
Quote: "Roll onto side after jerking stops OR immediately if food / fluid / vomit in mouth" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
- Epilepsy NZ web page: as soon as possible.
Quote: "onto their side as soon as possible" (Epilepsy NZ web)
- ANZCOR: lay them down, on the side when practical. St John: after it ends.
Quote: "Lay the person down and turn the person on the side when practical." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "As soon as the seizure ends" (St John)
Do: Loosen tight clothing. Wheelchair or stroller: brakes on.
Quote: "loosen any tight clothing" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "Make sure the wheelchair or the stroller is secure, by putting the brakes on." (Epilepsy NZ web)
Ask: Then?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Still going at 5 minutes -> five-min
- Call 111? See the list -> call-list
- They have epilepsy and a plan -> plan
- Confused, not jerking -> confused
- It has stopped -> after

### call-list: Call 111 now if
- In water, or they may be pregnant
Quote: "Seizure occurs in water" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "the person may be pregnant" (ANZCOR)
- Injured, or food, fluid or vomit in the mouth
Quote: "serious injury" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "There is food / fluid / vomit in mouth" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
- Breathing difficulty after the jerking stops, or not breathing well
Quote: "Person has breathing difficulties after jerking stops" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "not breathing well" (St John)
- Over 5 minutes, or another seizure follows
Quote: "Seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "Another seizure quickly follows" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Ask: Any apply?
- Yes: call 111 now -> called
- In water -> water
- None of these: more checks -> call-more

### call-more: Also call 111 if
- First seizure, you don't know them, or no plan
Quote: "this is their first seizure" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "you do not know the person" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "no seizure management plan" (ANZCOR)
- Not their usual pattern, or diabetes or another condition
Quote: "departs from the usual pattern" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "diabetes or an underlying health condition" (Epilepsy NZ web)
- You are in doubt, uneasy, or they ask for help
Quote: "You are in any doubt" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "You feel uncomfortable" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "asks for medical assistance" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Ask: Any apply?
- Yes: call 111 now -> called
- They have epilepsy and a plan -> plan
- None apply: still going -> during
- None apply: it has stopped -> after

### called: Call 111
Stop: After
Record: 111 called
Do: Stay with them and keep them safe. Tell the call taker it is a seizure and when it started (the Log has the times).
Quote: "Stay with the person" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Note: "Tell the call taker" is app wording; no source lists what to say on the call. Stop: After ends the not-waking bars once 111 is called.
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Still going -> during
- It has stopped -> after
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### five-min: 5 minutes: still going?
Warning: Still going after 5 minutes? Call 111 now. All the sources say this.
Quote: "Seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "the seizure does not stop after 5 minutes" (St John)
Quote: "the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "the seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes" (KidsHealth)
Say: Epilepsy NZ's web page adds: or follow the timing in their own plan.
Quote: "(or follow the timing in their personal SMP)" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Ask: Now?
- Call 111 -> called
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Their plan gives another time -> plan
- It has stopped -> after

### plan: Epilepsy with a plan
Do: Look for a MedicAlert pendant or bracelet. Follow their seizure plan if they have one.
Quote: "check for a MedicAlert ® pendant or bracelet" (St John)
Quote: "Follow the person's seizure management plan" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "follow any first aid advice" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Say: When to call 111 differs:
- St John: no ambulance unless it lasts over 5 minutes.
Quote: "no need for medical aid or an ambulance unless the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes" (St John)
- ANZCOR: call if it does not match their plan.
Quote: "not consistent with the person's prescribed management plan" (ANZCOR)
Say: Rescue medicine: ANZCOR says follow your training and their plan. No source gives more detail.
Quote: "follow your training and the person's seizure management plan" (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- Call 111 -> called
- Still going -> during
- It has stopped -> after

### water: Seizure in water
Do: Call 111 as soon as possible. Follow the call taker's instructions.
Quote: "(111) should always be called as soon as possible" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "Follow the instructions of the ambulance operator" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Do: Support their head: face above the water, head tilted back.
Quote: "face and head stays above the surface" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "Tilt the person's head back to ensure a clear airway" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "with the head tilted so the face is out of the water" (ANZCOR)
Say: When to take them out differs. Epilepsy NZ: once the movements stop. ANZCOR: as soon as it is safe. Others or a float can help.
Quote: "as soon as the active movements of the seizure have ceased" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Quote: "as soon it is safe to do so" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Assistance from others or use of a floatation device may be useful" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Say: They need a medical check even if they seem fine.
Quote: "they still require a medical assessment" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Out of the water, still going -> during
- Out, and it has stopped -> after
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### confused: Confused, not jerking
Do: Stay with them and reassure them. Gently guide them away from harm.
Quote: "Reassure and stay with the person until they have recovered" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "Gently guide away from harm" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Warning: Do not restrain them unless they are in danger.
Quote: "DO NOT restrain the person unless in danger" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Say: ANZCOR says this kind of seizure may turn into a full seizure.
Quote: "A Focal seizure may sometimes progress to a generalised seizure." (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Jerking started -> during
- Call 111? See the list -> call-list
- They have recovered -> handover

### not-breathing: Not breathing normally
Do: Call 111 now if nobody has.
Quote: "is not breathing well" (St John)
Record: 111 called
Say: ANZCOR: if they are unresponsive and not breathing normally, follow the Basic Life Support flowchart. These seizure sources give no more steps. Follow the 111 call taker.
Quote: "If unresponsive and not breathing normally, follow ANZCOR Basic life support flow chart." (ANZCOR)
Say: ANZCOR also says breathing may stop for a short time during a seizure. It does not say how long.
Quote: "shallow breathing or breathing may stop temporarily" (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- Breathing again, still seizing -> during
- Breathing again, it has stopped -> after
- Another seizure started -> another
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### after: It has stopped: check breathing
Stop: Seizure
Remind: after 5 minutes: After 5 min not responding? Call 111 (Epilepsy NZ, ANZCOR)
Quote: "non-responsive for more than 5 minutes after the seizure ends" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "does not regain consciousness within 5 minutes" (ANZCOR)
Remind: after 10 minutes: After 10 min not waking? Call 111 (St John)
Quote: "does not wake up after 10 minutes" (St John)
Do: Check their airway and that they are breathing normally.
Quote: "check their airway and make sure they are breathing normally" (St John)
Do: Breathing normally: roll them onto their side, head tilted back, chin up.
Quote: "If they are breathing normally, roll the person onto their side" (St John)
Quote: "their head tilted back so that their chin is away from their chest" (St John)
Ask: Breathing normally?
- Yes, they are on their side -> recovery
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Another seizure started -> another

### recovery: Stay with them
Do: Stay and reassure them until they have recovered. Cover them lightly.
Quote: "Reassure and stay with the person until they have recovered" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "Cover the person lightly" (St John)
Do: Keep checking their airway and breathing. Let them sleep, but check for a response every few minutes (no number given, so no reminder).
Quote: "frequently reassess the person, particularly their breathing" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "check for a response every few minutes" (St John)
Say: No source says if sleep counts as not responding. St John: unresponsive means they "cannot be woken up".
Quote: "cannot be woken up" (St John)
Warning: Nothing to eat or drink until fully awake and alert.
Quote: "Do not offer them anything to eat and drink until they are fully awake" (ANZCOR)
Ask: How are they?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Not responding -> unresponsive
- Waking up -> waking
- Another seizure started -> another

### unresponsive: Not waking up
Say: Call 111 if they stay unresponsive. The time differs:
- Epilepsy NZ and ANZCOR: more than 5 minutes after it stops.
Quote: "non-responsive for more than 5 minutes after the seizure ends" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "does not regain consciousness within 5 minutes" (ANZCOR)
- St John: not awake within 10 minutes.
Quote: "does not wake within 10 minutes" (St John)
- KidsHealth (children with fever): gives no time.
Quote: "your child is not waking up and responding after the seizure" (KidsHealth)
Say: The reminder bars count from when you tapped "It has stopped".
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Call 111 -> called
- Waking up -> waking
- Another seizure started -> another

### waking: As they wake
Stop: After
Do: Tell them they are safe. Check for injuries and give first aid. Keep onlookers away.
Quote: "Reassure the person as they wake up. Tell them they are safe." (St John)
Quote: "Check for injuries and apply any necessary first aid." (St John)
Quote: "Keep onlookers away" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Warning: No water, food or pills unless they are fully alert.
Quote: "Give the person water, food or pills unless they are fully alert." (Epilepsy NZ web)
Note: That Epilepsy NZ line sits under its "DO NOT" heading, so "No ..." keeps its meaning. Stop: After ends the not-waking bars once they wake.
Ask: Now?
- They have recovered -> handover
- Another seizure started -> another
- Not responding again -> unresponsive

### another: Another seizure started
Stop: all
Remind: after 5 minutes: Seizure 5 minutes: still going? Call 111
Quote: "Seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Warning: Call 111 now (Epilepsy NZ, ANZCOR, KidsHealth). St John does not list this.
Quote: "Another seizure quickly follows" (Epilepsy NZ poster)
Quote: "another seizure begins" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "If your child starts a second seizure before waking from the first one" (KidsHealth)
Say: No source gives a limit for a cluster of seizures. Keep them safe as before. The 5-minute bar restarts now.
Quote: "(or there is a cluster of seizures)" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Ask: Now?
- Call 111 -> called
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- In water -> water
- It has stopped -> after
- Child with a fever: it has stopped -> fever-after

### fever: Child with a fever
Do: Try to keep calm and keep your child safe. Loosen clothes at face and neck.
Quote: "try to keep calm, and keep your child safe" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "loosen their clothes around their face and neck" (KidsHealth)
Warning: Do not put them in a bath or shower to cool them (KidsHealth, ANZCOR). Nothing in their mouth.
Quote: "do not put your child in the bath or shower to cool them down" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "Put a child in a bath to lower their temperature" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "do not put anything in their mouth" (KidsHealth)
Say: St John differs: cool them by removing clothes down to underwear or nappy.
Quote: "removing their clothing down to underwear or nappy" (St John)
Say: On their side: KidsHealth "What to do" says now; its key points and St John say after it.
Quote: "lie your child down on their side" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "When the convulsion is over" (St John)
Quote: "after the seizure, lie your child down on their side" (KidsHealth)
Say: A KidsHealth picture (heading unreadable): video it if you can.
Quote: "Video the seizure if you can." (KidsHealth)
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Call 111? See the list -> fever-call
- It has stopped -> fever-after

### fever-call: Child: call 111 if
- It lasts longer than 5 minutes
Quote: "the seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes" (KidsHealth)
- Trouble breathing, or looks very unwell
Quote: "your child is having trouble breathing" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "your child looks very unwell" (KidsHealth)
- Another seizure before waking, or you are worried
Quote: "a second seizure before waking from the first one" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "or you are worried" (KidsHealth)
- Injured (ANZCOR, Epilepsy NZ)
Quote: "concerned about any aspect of the person's care such as injury" (ANZCOR)
Say: A KidsHealth picture (heading unreadable) also lists 2 or more seizures in a row, and injury.
Quote: "2 or more seizures in a row" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "a head injury or injured themselves" (KidsHealth)
Ask: Any apply?
- Yes: call 111 now -> called
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- None: still going -> fever
- None: it has stopped -> fever-after

### fever-after: Child: after it stops
Stop: Seizure
Remind: after 5 minutes: After 5 min not responding? Call 111 (ANZCOR)
Quote: "does not regain consciousness within 5 minutes" (ANZCOR)
Remind: after 10 minutes: After 10 min not waking? Call 111 (St John)
Quote: "does not wake up after 10 minutes" (St John)
Do: Check breathing, then lie them on their side in the recovery position. Keep checking their breathing.
Quote: "make sure they are breathing normally" (St John)
Quote: "lie your child down on their side, in the recovery position" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "Frequently reassess, particularly their airway and breathing" (ANZCOR)
Warning: Nothing to drink until fully conscious.
Quote: "DO NOT give the child anything to drink until they are fully conscious" (St John)
Say: KidsHealth gives no time for "not waking" and says they may sleep up to an hour; hard to wake: see a doctor urgently. No source says if sleep counts as not responding.
Quote: "(up to an hour)" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "if they are hard to wake" (KidsHealth)
Quote: "see a doctor urgently" (KidsHealth)
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Not waking and responding: call 111 -> called
- Another seizure started -> another
- Resting, recovering -> handover

### handover: Hand over
Stop: all
Say: Tell the ambulance or doctor: when it started and stopped, how many seizures, and when you called 111. The Log has the times.
Note: App wording; no source gives a handover list. Each answer (It has stopped, Another seizure started) and the 111 called button put a time in the Log.
Do: Advise them not to drive. Try to arrange someone to stay with them until they are home.
Quote: "Advise the person not to drive. Try to arrange for someone to be with them until they are safely home." (St John)
Say: No source says how long to stay once they have recovered.
Next: doctor

### doctor: See a doctor
Do: See a doctor after a first seizure, or a child after a fever seizure.
Quote: "person's first seizure, or if it's a child after a febrile" (St John)
Quote: "take your child to a health professional afterwards" (KidsHealth)
Say: Epilepsy: see their doctor to check their medicine (St John). After water: a medical check.
Quote: "check that any prescribed medication is adequate" (St John)
Quote: "they still require a medical assessment" (Epilepsy NZ web)
Say: Unsure? Healthline 0800 611 116 (St John). Epilepsy NZ advice: 0800 374 537.
Quote: "call Healthline on 0800 611 116" (St John)
Quote: "contact Epilepsy New Zealand on 0800 374 537" (Epilepsy NZ poster)

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch only (every site blocked download), so a quote not found may be the copy's fault. On the Epilepsy NZ poster, which column (DO / DON'T / CALL 111) a line sits under is inferred from layout, and the "call an ambulance 111 if" heading is a graphic. Lines in [square brackets] in source.txt are notes, never quoted. Quotes are short exact fragments to keep screens short.

Timing and records (review round 1): one clock only. The 5-minute reminder bar ("Seizure 5 minutes...") starts when the first screen opens and stays on every screen; the start screen says it counts from opening the screen, so a helper who opens the app late must allow for that. No Timer (a Timer only shows on its own screen and buzzed with the bar). The "5 minutes: still going?" screen (with Epilepsy NZ's "or follow the timing in their plan") is an answer on during, so it is always reachable; it is left off the start screen to keep that screen's answers to six, and off called and another, where 111 is already called or due. "It has stopped" stops the seizure bar (Stop: Seizure, a prefix match) and starts two not-waking bars, 5 minutes (Epilepsy NZ, ANZCOR; the child screen says ANZCOR only, as the Epilepsy NZ poster is for adults) and 10 minutes (St John). Both start with "After", so "Stop: After" on waking (they woke) and on called (111 is called) ends them; going back to "Not responding" from waking does not restart them (the times are shown on that screen). "Another seizure started" stops all bars and restarts the 5-minute bar. Records: only "111 called" (on called and not-breathing), because every other event is an answer that the Log already times; the Seizure started / stopped / another Record buttons were dropped as duplicate taps (review finding 13). St John's "check for a response every few minutes" has no number, so no reminder is set (said on screen). No source says whether a sleeping person counts as "non-responsive" or "not waking"; the recovery and child screens say so and quote St John's "cannot be woken up" rather than deciding.

Differences kept visible, never picked (lessons trap 5): (1) unresponsive after the seizure: 5 minutes (Epilepsy NZ poster and web, ANZCOR) vs 10 minutes (St John) vs no time (KidsHealth, children with fever); both bars run. (2) When to roll onto the side: poster after jerking stops or at once if food/fluid/vomit in the mouth (the "at once" case is also on the start screen, quoting the Epilepsy NZ web page); web page as soon as possible; ANZCOR lay down and on the side when practical; St John after it ends; KidsHealth's "What to do" says lie the child on their side, its key points (and St John for febrile) after the seizure; both on the fever screen. (3) Epilepsy with a plan: St John no ambulance unless over 5 minutes; Epilepsy NZ follow their plan and its timing; ANZCOR call if it does not match the plan. St John's MedicAlert check is on that screen. (4) Fever: St John cool by removing clothes to underwear or nappy; KidsHealth and ANZCOR never a bath (KidsHealth: or shower). ANZCOR's bath line sits under its "Do not:" heading. (5) Water: Epilepsy NZ remove once movements stop; ANZCOR as soon as safe. ANZCOR's copy reads "as soon it is safe" (sic). (6) Food and drink: ANZCOR (recovery), Epilepsy NZ web (waking, under its DO NOT heading), St John for a child (fever-after). (7) Restraint: St John's "DO NOT move the person, or try to restrain them" has no "unless"; Epilepsy NZ adds "unless in danger" and ANZCOR "unless this is essential to avoid injury"; the start warning keeps "unless in danger".

Call-111 list: two short screens (most urgent first: water, pregnant, injury, mouth, breathing, 5 minutes, another seizure; then first seizure, not known, no plan, pattern, other condition, doubt), each line quoted from the source that says it. Epilepsy NZ's "The person asks for medical assistance" is shortened to "they ask for help". The breathing line keeps the poster's "after jerking stops" and adds St John's "not breathing well". St John's own list is only 5 minutes, 10 minutes and not breathing well. KidsHealth infographic: its heading could not be read, so "2 or more seizures in a row", "head injury or injured themselves" and "Video the seizure" are labelled on screen as from a KidsHealth picture with an unreadable heading; the child injury line rests on ANZCOR's "such as injury".

Gaps shown on screen (trap 13): not breathing (only ANZCOR's pointer to the Basic Life Support flowchart; follow the 111 call taker); breathing may stop temporarily during a seizure (ANZCOR) with no time given; no limit for a cluster of seizures; how long to stay once they recover; rescue medicine (ANZCOR: follow training and the plan; no doses anywhere); no source says what to tell 111 or paramedics (handover wording is the app's); KidsHealth gives no time for "not waking"; whether sleep counts as not responding.
A child's second seizure goes to the adult "It has stopped" screen unless the helper picks "Child with a fever: it has stopped" on the another-seizure screen.
Left out on purpose: background (what to look for, febrile seizure facts) except "sleep up to an hour" and ANZCOR's focal-to-generalised line; St John's bystander-reassurance line; the rest of Epilepsy NZ's seated-position section and its focal, absence and atonic sections were not captured.
