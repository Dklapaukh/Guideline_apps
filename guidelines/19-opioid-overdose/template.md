# Opioid overdose

About: Helps a bystander in New Zealand when someone may have overdosed on opioids: the signs, calling 111, giving naloxone from a kit (or what to do with no kit), waiting and repeating a dose, rescue breathing, staying with them, and the dose times the paramedics need.

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch only (healthify.nz and medsafe.govt.nz downloads blocked; see meta.json), so a quote not found may be the copy's fault. Lines in [square brackets] in source.txt are notes, never quoted (so the unconfirmed Healthify line "An overdose is always an emergency..." is not used; the banner's "An overdose is an emergency" rests on Healthify 'Opioid overdose' C1). NZ sources first; every Australian line says "Australian" on screen. Nyxoid (nasal spray) is not available in NZ (Healthify A2, The Level E); its lines are used only to show differences.

Call order: 111 comes first on the start screen because Healthify 'Opioid overdose' (C1: "Call 111 for help if you suspect someone has overdosed on opioids") and The Level (step 1, call, before step 2, safe place) put it first. The NZ kit pamphlet puts safety first (step 1) and the call second (step 2); health.gov.au (Australian) lists naloxone first, then call 000. The safety check is on the next screen. Stay with them (Healthify A4, C3) is on the start screen so the kit route has it before they wake.

Wait timer (lessons traps 1 and 6): the wait before another dose is 2 to 5 minutes (Healthify, main), 3-5 minutes (kit pamphlet), wait 2-3 minutes and repeat if still unconscious after 3 minutes (The Level); 2 to 3 minutes for Nyxoid (Medsafe; not in NZ) and NSW (Australian, a different product). The Timer is 3 minutes because it is the only value inside all three NZ injection sources. The screen after the timer (check) asks whether they are waking and breathing; it never re-doses automatically, and shows each source's rule for another dose. Both wait screens (first dose and repeat doses) use 3 minutes and restart on each visit. The breathe screen's "Still not breathing, I have a kit: another dose" goes straight to another dose without a timer, because NSW (Australian) says repeat every 2 to 3 minutes if not recovering and the kit says to keep giving naloxone; check says to look at the last dose time first.

20-minute bar: one once-only reminder, on the first wait screen (wait-first), so it starts as the helper leaves the first dose screen, about when the first dose is given (the runtime cannot start a bar from a Record tap). Repeat doses go through again and wait, which have no bar, so it never restarts on later doses. If the helper reaches wait-first twice (only by Back), a bar that has already been dismissed can start again; this is a small runtime limit. Its text says "about now". Durations differ: 20 minutes (Healthify A4, kit pamphlet), 20 to 90 minutes (Healthify A5, same page), about 30-90 / 30 to 90 minutes (NSW, health.gov.au, Australian); the captured part of the Medsafe leaflet gives no time. All are shown on the woke screen; the bar uses 20 minutes, the shortest NZ time.

Number of doses: Healthify and the kit pamphlet describe a second dose only; The Level repeat as many times as needed, no maximum; Medsafe further doses every 2-3 minutes; NSW repeat until recovered. The loop (check -> again -> wait -> check) lets the helper keep going, and the Record count shows the number. The kit has 5 ampoules; the runtime can't branch on a count, so check has a "No ampoules left" answer to the no-kit screen; no source says what to do when they run out (said on screen; 111 can advise, Healthify C3).

Recovery position order: Healthify A4 and the kit pamphlet (step 3, before naloxone at step 4) put them on their side first; The Level's injection steps have none; Nyxoid (The Level, Medsafe) lays them on their back for the spray and then on their side; NSW (Australian) once they recover. The app follows the NZ kit pamphlet (injectable is the only NZ form) and says on the safe screen that others differ; NSW's recovery-position line is on the woke screen.

Ampoule dot (guideline contradiction): the kit pamphlet says turn the dot away from you, then push to snap the top off (it does not say which way to push); The Level says hold the blue dot facing towards you and push the top away from you. Both shown, the kit pamphlet first, with Healthify A3's "see the leaflet in the emergency kit" as the tie-break. Dose amount: no NZ source states how much to draw up or inject (the kit pamphlet's "(see below)" injection text was not captured; The Level says push the plunger all the way down, which implies the whole ampoule but does not say so). NSW Prenoxad "0.4 ml to the first black line" is a different pre-filled Australian product, so it is left out.

Rescue breathing: "2 breaths every 5 seconds" (Healthify A4, kit pamphlet step 6) quoted as is. When: Healthify after the second dose; kit pamphlet if not breathing (after the first dose); Healthify C3 (no kit) if 111 asks. NZ sources give no technique and do not say how to position the person (they were put on their side); said on screen, with NSW (Australian) steps labelled, "if you know how", "2 quick breaths" (differs from "2 breaths every 5 seconds"). CPR: no NZ source gives CPR steps; The Level and NSW say CPR if you know how; "follow the 111 call-taker" rests on Healthify C3. Guideline contradiction: the kit pamphlet says "naloxone is not effective against respiratory depression", while Healthify says naloxone wearing off "could cause breathing to stop again" (so naloxone keeps breathing going) and C4 says it "can reverse the effects of opioids". The sources do not explain this; the screen shows both and keeps both actions (keep giving naloxone, breathe for them), without guessing the meaning (trap 5).

After waking: The Level "That person may need medical help after you give them naloxone", and health.gov.au (Australian) "It is vital to call an ambulance", so the woke screen says keep the ambulance coming. Gap: no source says what to do if the person wants to leave or refuses the ambulance; follow the 111 call-taker.

Handover: the only thing NZ sources say to tell paramedics is the time(s) naloxone was given (Healthify A4, kit steps 5 and 7); the rest of the Log list is the app's wording, said on screen. NSW's "hand the syringe to the crew" is Australian (Prenoxad), labelled. Record buttons: 111 called (start only, so the count means calls), Recovery position (no-kit, safe, woke), Naloxone dose given (inject and again: one shared count and time), Rescue breathing started, Person woke up, Naloxone wore off.

Left out on purpose: risk factors (kit pamphlet B3), kit storage, cautions ("not a safety net"), where to get kits, rare serious side effects after a large dose (background), The Level's full sign list (Healthify's is used), Nyxoid spray method (not available in NZ).
