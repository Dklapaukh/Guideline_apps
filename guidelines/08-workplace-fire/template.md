# Fire at work

About: Helps you act in a fire at work: warn others, get out, call 111 and ask for FIRE, and decide whether it is safe to use a fire extinguisher. Based on Fire and Emergency New Zealand (FENZ) advice.
Source: Fire and Emergency New Zealand, Fire Action Notice - https://www.fireandemergency.nz/assets/Documents/EvacuationSchemes/Fire-Action-Notice-English-handwritten.pdf
Source: Fire and Emergency New Zealand, Example Evacuation Scheme Document (office building exemplar) - https://www.fireandemergency.nz/assets/Documents/EvacuationSchemes/Office-Building-exemplar-supporting-documents.pdf
Source: Fire and Emergency New Zealand, Our 3-Step Escape Plan and business fire safety checklist - https://www.fireandemergency.nz/building-fire-safety/business-fire-safety-checklist/
Source: Fire and Emergency New Zealand, Commercial use of fire extinguishers - https://fireandemergency.nz/business-and-landlords/commercial-use-of-fire-extinguishers
Source: Fire and Emergency New Zealand, Fire extinguisher position document - https://www.fireandemergency.nz/assets/Documents/Business-and-Landlords/Protecting-your-business-or-Property/Fire-Extinguisher-Position.pdf
Source: Fire and Emergency New Zealand, Fire extinguishers (home fire safety) - https://www.fireandemergency.nz/mi_NZ/home-fire-safety/specialised-safety-devices/fire-extinguishers/
Version: Fire and Emergency New Zealand Fire Action Notice, evacuation scheme exemplar and fire extinguisher pages (no dates shown; site footer © 2026), read 19 Nov 2026
Audience: Anyone at work in New Zealand who finds a fire or hears the fire alarm
Warning: In a fire, get out and stay out. Call 111 and ask for FIRE.
Reviewed by:

## Steps

### start: What's happening?
Ask: What do you need?
- There's a fire, or the alarm is going -> warn
- Should I use the fire extinguisher? -> ext-small
- Get ready: what to know before a fire -> prep-notice

### warn: Warn others and call 111
Do: Shout "Fire, Fire, Fire" to warn people near you.
Quote: "WARN OTHER BUILDING OCCUPANTS" (Fire Action Notice)
Quote: "Warn occupants in the immediate area by shouting “Fire, Fire, Fire”" (Example evacuation scheme)
Do: If the alarm isn't going, set it off at the nearest call point: a red box with a break-glass panel.
Quote: "Activate the nearest manual call point if the alarm is not already sounding" (Example evacuation scheme)
Quote: "these are red boxes with break-glass panels" (Example evacuation scheme)
Do: Call 111 from a safe phone and ask for FIRE. If you can't safely, call as soon as you are out.
Quote: "DIAL 111 FROM A SAFE PHONE – ASK FOR FIRE" (Fire Action Notice)
Quote: "Call emergency services as soon as you are out and safe." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Next: leave

### leave: Get out now
Warning: Leave the building now, by the nearest exit.
Quote: "YOU MUST LEAVE THE BUILDING IMMEDIATELY USING THE NEAREST EXIT" (Fire Action Notice)
Say: Be fast. Don't stop for your things.
Quote: "Be fast" (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "Don't spend time trying to save possessions." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Do: Check the area you're in for other people. Help anyone who needs it on the way out.
Quote: "Check the area you are in for other people" (Example evacuation scheme, after hours)
Quote: "Evacuate the building, assisting anyone else you see on the way" (Example evacuation scheme, after hours)
Quote: "Assist others to evacuate as required" (Example evacuation scheme)
Do: On an upper floor, use the stairs.
Quote: "Evacuate the building, if on upper floors use the stairs" (Example evacuation scheme)
Say: Lifts: FENZ doesn't say. Follow your workplace's plan.
Note: Lifts are a gap (none of the FENZ pages read mention them). The after-hours lines are general actions (not warden duties), so they are used here.
Next: smoke

### smoke: On the way out
Do: Get low if there is smoke. Smoke is more deadly than flames.
Quote: "Get low" (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "Smoke is poisonous and more deadly than flames." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Do: Close doors behind you. A closed door buys you time.
Quote: "Close doors" (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "A closed door buys you time." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Note: "Get low" and "Close doors" come from FENZ's 3-Step Escape Plan, written for homes but shown on FENZ's business pages. The office example only lists closing doors as a training topic. The provenance is explained in the Get ready route and in Notes, not here.
Next: call

### call: Once you're out
Do: If 111 hasn't been called yet, call now and ask for FIRE.
Quote: "Call emergency services as soon as you are out and safe." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "Ask for FIRE." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Do: Give the address clearly. Say if anyone is still inside or missing.
Quote: "Clearly state your address and let the operator know it anyone is still inside or has not made it to the safe meeting place." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Next: assemble

### assemble: Go to the assembly point
Do: Go to your workplace's assembly point.
Quote: "ASSEMBLE" (Fire Action Notice)
Say: It should be on the Fire Action Notice on your wall.
Note: The Fire Action Notice leaves the assembly point blank for each building to fill in. The example scheme's grass area and flagpole belong to that made-up building only, so they are not shown.
Next: stay-out

### stay-out: Stay out
Warning: Don't go back inside for any reason. People have died going back into a fire.
Quote: "Don't leave the meeting place to go back inside for any reason." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "People have died by going back into a fire." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Say: Your workplace's plan says who can give the all-clear.
Note: App wording. In FENZ's example scheme the all-clear is given by "the Head Warden or Fire and Emergency" (Area Wardens' duty), but that is the made-up building's set-up, so it isn't quoted as the user's instruction.
End: You did the right thing by getting out.

### ext-small: Is the fire small?
Warning: First, shout "Fire, Fire, Fire" and set off the fire alarm.
Quote: "WARN OTHER BUILDING OCCUPANTS" (Fire Action Notice)
Quote: "Operate the nearest fire alarm manual call point" (Example evacuation scheme)
Say: Only use an extinguisher if it's safe. Life is more important than property.
Quote: "Only use fire extinguishers when it is safe to do so" (Commercial use of fire extinguishers)
Quote: "Remember, life is more important than property." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Say: The fire must be no bigger than a waste paper basket or a pot on a stove, and in a place that is safe to get near.
Quote: "when the fire is no larger than a waste paper basket or pot on a stove." (Commercial use of fire extinguishers)
Quote: "It's safe to do so considering the size and location of the fire" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Ask: Is the fire that small, and safe to get near?
- Yes -> ext-escape
- No, or I'm not sure -> get-out

### ext-escape: Can you get away safely?
Say: You need a safe way out, so you can back away from the fire.
Quote: "Always have a safe path of escape" (Commercial use of fire extinguishers)
Quote: "You can safely access and retreat from the fire." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Ask: Do you have a safe way out?
- Yes -> ext-called
- No, or I'm not sure -> get-out

### ext-called: Is help on the way?
Say: 111 must have been called, and everyone else must be out.
Quote: "ensure Fire and Emergency has been called." (Commercial use of fire extinguishers)
Quote: "Everyone has been evacuated and accounted for at your safe meeting place." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Note: "Everyone is out" comes from FENZ's home page; the commercial page only asks for a safe escape path and FENZ called. The stricter rule is kept. Two conditions in one question to keep the route short; both must be yes.
Ask: Has 111 been called, and is everyone else out?
- Yes, both -> ext-type
- No, or I'm not sure -> get-out

### ext-type: Is it the right extinguisher?
Warning: Gas fire: turn off the gas supply first.
Quote: "Always turn off the supply of gas before extinguishing gas fires." (Fire extinguisher position document)
Warning: Cooking oil or fat: never use dry powder. Live electricity: don't use wet chemical.
Quote: "you should never use it on a cooking oil or fat fire" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Quote: "However, don't use wet chemical extinguishers on fires with a live electrical source." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Say: Metal fires need a special extinguisher.
Quote: "Special purpose extinguishers are available for metal fires." (Fire extinguisher position document)
Note: FENZ's table of which extinguisher suits which class could not be read, so this screen gives only the pairings FENZ states in words. The question is backed by "confident you understand how to use the extinguisher correctly" (home page). The gap is explained in the Get ready route.
Ask: Are you sure this extinguisher is right for this fire?
- Yes -> ext-confident
- No, or I'm not sure -> get-out

### ext-confident: Do you know how?
Say: Only go ahead if you're confident you know how to use it.
Quote: "You're confident you understand how to use the extinguisher correctly." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Ask: Are you confident you know how to use it?
- Yes -> ext-use
- No, or I'm not sure -> get-out

### ext-use: Use it: PTASS
Say: It only lasts 10-15 seconds once started.
Quote: "your extinguisher will only last 10-15 seconds once started" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Do: Pull the safety pin or remove the clip.
Quote: "Pull the safety pin or remove the clip." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Do: Test squirt it to check it works.
Quote: "Test squirt the extinguisher to make sure it is working." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Do: Aim at the base of the fire from a safe distance (most extinguishers work from about 2-3 metres).
Quote: "Aim the nozzle at the base of the fire from a safe distance." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Quote: "Most extinguishers are designed to be operated from about 2 - 3 metres away." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Do: Squeeze the handles.
Quote: "Squeeze the handles." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Do: Sweep from side to side, aiming at the base of the fire.
Quote: "Sweep the extinguisher from side to side while aiming at the base of the fire." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Note: No Timer: 10-15 seconds is a range (lessons trap 1) and a countdown would pull eyes off the fire; it is shown as a fact. The PTASS steps are on FENZ's home page; the commercial page links to it ("See our advice on how to use a fire exitnguisher.").
Ask: Is the fire out?
- Yes, it's out -> ext-done
- No, it's still burning -> get-out

### ext-done: The fire is out
Say: Well done.
Say: These FENZ pages don't say what to do once a fire is out, such as whether to stay nearby. Follow your workplace's procedure, and tell Fire and Emergency what happened when they arrive.
Note: App wording for a gap in the sources (no aftercare: whether to stay, leave, refill or report the extinguisher). "Liaise with Fire and Emergency upon their arrival" is not quoted because in the example it follows evacuating after hours, not putting a fire out.
End:

### get-out: Don't fight it. Get out.
Warning: Stop fighting the fire. Leave now.
Quote: "Don't put yourself or others at risk." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Say: Life is more important than property.
Quote: "Remember, life is more important than property." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Note: Every "no" in the extinguisher check, and "still burning", comes here, then joins the main fire route at its first screen (warn others, alarm, 111), so the person who found the fire never skips raising the alarm.
Next: warn

### prep-notice: Read your Fire Action Notice
Say: Your workplace should have a Fire Action Notice. Find it and learn:
Quote: "FIRE ACTION NOTICE" (Fire Action Notice)
Do: Where the exits are.
Quote: "EXITS ARE LOCATED" (Fire Action Notice)
Do: Where the extinguishers and other firefighting equipment are.
Quote: "FIREFIGHTING EQUIPMENT IS LOCATED" (Fire Action Notice)
Do: Where to assemble.
Quote: "ASSEMBLE" (Fire Action Notice)
Do: What to do if you need help to get out.
Quote: "IF YOU REQUIRE ASSISTANCE TO EVACUATE" (Fire Action Notice)
Say: FENZ leaves these blank for each building to fill in, so this app can't tell you yours.
Note: The FENZ notice is a form; these lines are blanks. "What to do" is app wording for the blank after "IF YOU REQUIRE ASSISTANCE TO EVACUATE".
Next: prep-plan

### prep-plan: Know your way out
Do: Know a first and a second way out, and where to meet.
Quote: "First Escape Route" (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "Second Escape Route" (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "Meeting Place" (3-Step Escape Plan)
Do: Learn your workplace's evacuation scheme, so you can follow it in an emergency.
Quote: "Your employees understand the evacuation scheme and can action it in an emergency." (Business fire safety checklist)
Say: Be fast in a fire. A house fire can kill in less than three minutes.
Quote: "Be fast" (3-Step Escape Plan)
Quote: "A house fire can kill you in less than three minutes." (3-Step Escape Plan)
Say: The advice to get low, close doors and stay out comes from FENZ's home escape plan, which FENZ also shows to businesses. FENZ lists closing doors and using extinguishers as workplace training topics, so ask about them at work.
Quote: "Importance of closing doors to restrict fire spread" (Example evacuation scheme, training topics)
Quote: "Use of fire extinguishers" (Example evacuation scheme, training topics)
Say: These FENZ pages say nothing about lifts. Check your workplace's plan.
Note: "A house fire" is kept as printed rather than applied to workplaces. "Ask about them at work" is app wording.
Next: prep-workplace

### prep-workplace: What your workplace should have
Say: FENZ's business checklist says a workplace should have:
Quote: "Business fire safety checklist" (Business fire safety checklist)
- Monitored fire alarms connected to Fire and Emergency.
Quote: "You have monitored fire alarms that are connected to the Fire and Emergency New Zealand Communication Centres." (Business fire safety checklist)
- Fire extinguishers, and key staff trained to use them.
Quote: "You have fire extinguishers in the building and key staff have been trained to use them." (Business fire safety checklist)
- A working fire hose reel that emergency services can reach.
Quote: "You have a working fire hose reel in the building that can be accessed and used by emergency services." (Business fire safety checklist)
- An evacuation scheme approved by Fire and Emergency.
Quote: "You have an evacuation scheme that has been approved by Fire and Emergency New Zealand." (Business fire safety checklist)
Note: Shown as a list, not tick-box actions, because these are the business's to provide. No "tell your manager" advice is added (FENZ doesn't say).
Next: prep-types

### prep-types: Kinds of extinguisher
Say: There are 6 classes of fire. The right extinguisher depends on the class of fire you're most likely to have.
Quote: "The type of fire extinguisher you need depends on the class of fire you're most likely to experience." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Quote: "There are 6 classes of fire:" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
- A: wood, paper, plastics
Quote: "Class A (Wood paper plastics)" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
- B: flammable liquids, e.g. paint, petrol, oil
Quote: "Class B: Flammable liquids e.g. paint, petrol, oil" (Commercial use of fire extinguishers)
- C: flammable gases, e.g. LPG
Quote: "Class C: Flammable gases e.g. butane, CNG, LPG" (Commercial use of fire extinguishers)
- D: metals, e.g. magnesium
Quote: "Class D: Flammable metals e.g. titanium, magnesium" (Commercial use of fire extinguishers)
- E: electrical equipment
Quote: "Class E (Electrically energised equipment)" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
- F: cooking oils and fats
Quote: "Class F (Cooking oils and fats)" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Say: Wet chemical is best for cooking oil and fat fires, but don't use it where there is live electricity.
Quote: "A Wet Chemical extinguisher is best for extinguishing cooking oil and fat fires." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Quote: "However, don't use wet chemical extinguishers on fires with a live electrical source." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Warning: Never use dry powder on a cooking oil or fat fire: it spreads the fire.
Quote: "you should never use it on a cooking oil or fat fire" (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Quote: "the pressure from a dry powder extinguisher will cause the fire to spread." (Fire extinguishers, home page)
Say: FENZ has a table of which extinguisher suits which class, but this copy of it couldn't be read. Check the label on your extinguishers, or the manufacturer's instructions.
Quote: "For more information, refer to the manufacturer’s instructions or New Zealand Standard NZS 4503:2005" (Fire extinguisher position document)

End:

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch grade (meta.json). fireandemergency.nz is blocked here; a fetch tool quoted short pieces, so a few words may differ from FENZ's originals. FENZ's own slips are kept inside quotes ("it anyone", "exitnguisher"). Saving the FENZ PDFs into sources/ would make checking exact.
Six FENZ parts are combined: the Fire Action Notice (general, but its exits, equipment, assembly point and assistance lines are blanks each building fills in); the example office evacuation scheme (a made-up building: its grass area, flagpole, "dial 1 for an outside line" and Building/Area/Head Warden roles are not presented as the user's own); the 3-Step Escape Plan (written for homes, shown on FENZ business pages); the commercial extinguisher page (when to use one); the extinguisher position PDF (gas, metal fires); and the home extinguisher page (conditions, 10-15 seconds, PTASS, types and classes).
Order of 111: the Fire Action Notice puts "WARN OTHER BUILDING OCCUPANTS & DIAL 111 FROM A SAFE PHONE" together, and the example calls 111 before evacuating, so the first fire screen has warn, alarm and 111 together. The 3-Step Plan's "as soon as you are out and safe" covers anyone who can't call safely inside; the "Once you're out" screen repeats the call for them and adds the address and who is missing.
Example-scheme lines used as general actions: shouting "Fire, Fire, Fire", the call point, the stairs, checking your area for people and helping anyone on the way (the last two from its after-hours section). Warden duties are not used: "if in any doubt, make another 111 call", reporting to the Building Warden, and who gives the all-clear (the example says the Head Warden or Fire and Emergency). The stay-out screen says the workplace plan names who gives the all-clear.
Extinguisher route: it starts by reminding the person to shout and set off the alarm. Every question must be yes; any "no" or "not sure", or a fire still burning after use, goes to a "Stop fighting the fire" screen and then to the first fire screen (warn, alarm, 111). Size and location are asked together ("size and location of the fire", home page, plus the commercial page's waste paper basket). "Everyone has been evacuated and accounted for" is the home page's condition and is stricter than the commercial page; it is kept. "Right extinguisher" and "know how to use it" are separate one-question screens.
No Timer on the extinguisher screen: "10-15 seconds" is a range (lessons trap 1), it is a fact about the extinguisher rather than a wait, and a countdown would distract. Shown as Say. "About 2 - 3 metres" keeps "most extinguishers" and "a safe distance".
Close doors: the instruction is shown as given in the 3-Step Escape Plan. That plan is written for homes; the office example lists closing doors as a training topic with no instructions. This is explained on the Get ready route, not during the fire.
Gaps (said on screen where relevant): lifts (none of the FENZ pages mention them; the example only says to use the stairs from upper floors); closing doors at work specifically (see above); which extinguisher suits which class (table marks unreadable); what to do after a fire is put out (whether to stay, leave, report); what to do if the assembly point is unsafe (only the example building's flagpole is given, so it is left out).
Left out on purpose: "dial 1 for an outside line" (the example building's phones); "Liaise with Fire and Emergency upon their arrival" as a quote (it follows evacuating after hours, not putting a fire out); the 80% / 90% statistics (background, not instructions).
Class list: the home page and the commercial table word the classes slightly differently; the app's short list takes examples from both and quotes each line used.
