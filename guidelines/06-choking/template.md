# Adult choking

About: Guides you through helping an adult who is choking: checking their cough, back blows and chest thrusts, and what to do if they become unresponsive.
Source: ANZCOR Guideline 4 - Airway, section 4 (Management of Foreign Body Airway Obstruction) - https://www.anzcor.org/home/basic-life-support/guideline-4-airway
Version: ANZCOR Guideline 4, approved April 2021 (PDF current as of 22 May 2024), read 19 Nov 2026
Audience: Anyone helping an adult who is choking
Warning: Choking is a life-threatening emergency. Call an ambulance early.
Reviewed by:

## Steps

### start: Is their cough working?
Warning: Choking is a life-threatening emergency.
Quote: "A Foreign Body Airway Obstruction (FBAO) is a life-threatening emergency."
Say: A choking person may clutch their neck with their thumb and fingers. Their breathing may be laboured or noisy, or they may try to breathe with no sound of breathing and no air from the nose or mouth.
Quote: "This may progress to the universal choking sign, namely clutching the neck with the thumb and fingers"
Quote: "breathing is labored"
Quote: "breathing may be noisy"
Quote: "there may be efforts at breathing"
Quote: "there is no sound of breathing"
Quote: "there is no escape of air from nose and/or mouth."
Note: The guideline lists these signs under partial and complete obstruction; the headings were not captured, so the app lists the signs without them. "some escape of air can be felt from the mouth" (a partial-obstruction sign) is left out to keep the screen short.
Say: The simplest check is whether they can cough well. The guideline does not describe what a good cough looks like.
Quote: "The simplest way to assess severity of a FBAO is to assess for effective cough."
Ask: Can they cough well?
- Yes, they are coughing well -> mild
- No, their cough isn't working (they are still awake) -> back-blows
- They are unresponsive -> unresponsive

### mild: Keep them coughing
Do: Reassure them and encourage them to keep coughing to bring the object up.
Quote: "The person with an effective cough should be given reassurance and encouragement to keep coughing"
Quote: "to expel the foreign material."
Say: If it doesn't clear, call an ambulance. The guideline does not say how long to wait, so if you are unsure, call.
Quote: "If the obstruction is not relieved the rescuer should call an ambulance."
Note: "if you are unsure, call" is app wording for the gap (lessons trap 13), pointing back to the guideline's own instruction.
Ask: What is happening now?
- It has cleared -> cleared
- It hasn't cleared, but they are still coughing well -> mild-call
- Their cough has stopped working -> back-blows
- They are unresponsive -> unresponsive

### mild-call: Call an ambulance
Do: Call an ambulance.
Quote: "If the obstruction is not relieved the rescuer should call an ambulance."
Do: Keep reassuring them and encouraging them to cough.
Quote: "The person with an effective cough should be given reassurance and encouragement to keep coughing"
Ask: What is happening now?
- It has cleared -> cleared
- Their cough has stopped working -> back-blows
- They are unresponsive -> unresponsive
- The ambulance has arrived -> handover

### back-blows: Up to 5 back blows
Do: Send for an ambulance now, if nobody has yet.
Quote: "If the person is conscious send for an ambulance and perform up to five sharp, back blows"
Say: Abdominal thrusts are not recommended. Use back blows and chest thrusts, one after the other, until it clears.
Quote: "Therefore, the use of abdominal thrusts in the management of FBAO is not recommended"
Quote: "These techniques should be applied in rapid sequence until the obstruction is relieved."
Do: Give up to five sharp back blows with your hand, in the middle of the back between the shoulder blades.
Quote: "If the person is conscious send for an ambulance and perform up to five sharp, back blows"
Quote: "with the hand in the middle of the back between the shoulder blades."
Do: Check after each blow whether it has cleared.
Quote: "Check to see if each back blow has relieved the airway obstruction."
Say: Aim to clear it with each blow. Stop as soon as it clears; you don't have to give all five.
Quote: "The aim is to relieve the obstruction with each blow rather than to give all five blows."
Say: The guideline does not say how an adult should be positioned for back blows (it says they can sit or stand for chest thrusts).
Note: Gap in the guideline (lessons trap 13): it gives positions for infants (back blows) and for chest thrusts only.
Ask: Has it cleared?
- Yes, it has cleared -> cleared
- No, and they are still responsive -> chest-thrusts
- They have become unresponsive -> unresponsive
- The ambulance has arrived -> handover

### chest-thrusts: Up to 5 chest thrusts
Do: Find the same spot on the chest as for CPR and give up to five chest thrusts.
Quote: "If back blows are unsuccessful the rescuer should perform up to five chest thrusts."
Quote: "To perform chest thrusts, identify the same compression point as for CPR and give up to five chest thrusts."
Say: They are like chest compressions, but sharper and slower.
Quote: "These are similar to chest compressions but sharper and delivered at a slower rate."
Say: The person can be sitting or standing.
Quote: "while children and adults may be treated in the sitting or standing position."
Say: This guideline does not describe where the CPR compression point is.
Do: Check after each thrust whether it has cleared.
Quote: "With each chest thrust, check to see whether the airway obstruction has been relieved."
Say: Aim to clear it. Stop as soon as it clears; you don't have to give all five.
Quote: "The aim is to relieve the obstruction rather than deliver all five chest thrusts."
Say: If it hasn't cleared and they are still responsive, keep alternating five back blows with five chest thrusts.
Quote: "If the obstruction is still not relieved and the person remains responsive,"
Quote: "continue alternating five back blows with five chest thrusts."
Ask: Has it cleared?
- Yes, it has cleared -> cleared
- No, and they are still responsive: back to back blows -> back-blows
- They have become unresponsive -> unresponsive
- The ambulance has arrived -> handover

### unresponsive: They are unresponsive
Warning: Avoid blind finger sweeps inside their mouth: the guideline suggests against them.
Quote: "ANZCOR suggests against the use of blind finger sweeps."
Do: If you can see something in their mouth, consider taking it out by hand.
Quote: "ANZCOR suggests that rescuers consider the manual extraction of visible items in the mouth."
Do: Make sure an ambulance has been called.
Note: Not quoted for this step; the guideline's ambulance lines are in 4.2 and 4.3 (conscious person). Kept so the route never skips the ambulance.
Warning: If they are unresponsive and not breathing normally, follow the ANZCOR Basic Life Support Flowchart (Guideline 8). This app does not include that flowchart.
Quote: "If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, follow ANZCOR Basic Life Support Flowchart (Guideline 8)."
Say: The choking section does not say what to do if they are unresponsive but breathing normally. Follow the advice of the ambulance call taker.
Note: Gap in the guideline's choking section (lessons trap 13).
End:

### cleared: It has cleared
Say: Well done. The blockage has cleared.
Say: The choking section of the guideline does not say what to do after the blockage clears, for example whether the person should be checked by a doctor. If an ambulance is on the way, follow the advice of the ambulance staff.
Note: Gap in the guideline (lessons trap 13): no aftercare is given in section 4.
End:

### handover: The ambulance has arrived
Say: Tell the ambulance staff what happened and what you have done, and follow their advice.
Note: App wording; the guideline gives no handover step. Added so the back-blow and chest-thrust loop always has a way out.
End:

## Notes
Source copy: anzcor.org is blocked from this workspace; the PDF was read through a fetch tool that quotes only short pieces, so long sentences are split into several Quote lines. Figure 4 (the Choking Algorithm) is an image and could not be read; the app follows the written sections 3.0 to 4.3. If the algorithm adds anything, a later run should add it once the PDF is in sources/.
Order of techniques: section 4.0 says there is not enough evidence to say whether back blows or chest thrusts should come first ("More than one technique may be needed: there is insufficient evidence to determine which should be used first."). The app gives back blows first because section 4.3 describes them first ("If back blows are unsuccessful the rescuer should perform up to five chest thrusts."). Lessons trap 5: the specific instruction is followed.
"Call an ambulance": the guideline does not give a phone number (it is an Australian and New Zealand guideline), so the app does not add one.
Adults only: infant positions in 4.3 are left out because this app is for adults. Children share the chest-thrust position with adults, but the app is not labelled for children.
Gaps: no description of an effective cough; no time limit before calling an ambulance for a mild obstruction; no adult position for back blows; no CPR compression point in this guideline; nothing for an unresponsive person who is breathing normally; no aftercare once the blockage clears. Each is said on screen.
