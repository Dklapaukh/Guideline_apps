# Burns first aid

About: Walks a first aider through calling for help, cooling a burn for 20 minutes and covering it.
Source: Hato Hone St John first aid library, Burns - https://www.stjohn.org.nz/first-aid/first-aid-library/burns/
Version: St John web page, read 18 Nov 2026 (no review date shown)
Audience: Anyone giving first aid for a burn
Warning: If you have a person in urgent need of medical attention, call 111 now.
Reviewed by:

## Steps

### start: What caused the burn?
Say: Check that the person is happy for you to touch them before you give first aid.
Quote: "Check that the person is happy for you to touch them before you give first aid"
Say: If they are unconscious and need lifesaving first aid, don't delay.
Note: Reworded from the same Quick help item; no exact quote captured.
Say: A dry cough can mean smoke or fire has damaged the airway: choose "Smoke or gas".
Note: Reworded from "What to look for"; no exact quote captured.
Ask: What caused the burn?
- Heat, hot liquid or a hot object -> call-check
- A chemical (or bitumen) -> chemical
Note: Electricity is named on the page as a cause, but it gives no separate steps, so there is no answer for it.
- Smoke or gas: burns in the mouth or throat -> smoke
- Clothing is on fire -> on-fire

### call-check: Does this need an ambulance?
Say: Call 111 for an ambulance if any of these is true:
- The burn is larger than half the person's arm.
Quote: "Call 111 for an ambulance if the area of the burn is larger than half the person’s arm;"
- The burn causes severe pain.
- The burn involves the eyes.
Note: Severe pain and eyes are from the same Quick help list; only the "half the person's arm" line was captured word for word.
Ask: Is any of these true?
- Yes: call 111, then start cooling -> call-111
- No -> cool

### call-111: Call 111
Do: Call 111 for an ambulance.
Quote: "Call 111 for an ambulance if the area of the burn is larger than half the person’s arm;"
Say: Start cooling the burn while you wait.
Next: cool

### chemical: Chemical burn
Warning: Call 111 for an ambulance urgently.
Quote: "Call 111 for an ambulance urgently."
Do: Call 111.
Do: Remove contaminated clothing. If the chemical is a powder, brush it off carefully.
Note: Reworded from the page; no exact quote captured.
Do: Soak the area with lots of water.
Quote: "Soak the area with lots of water"
Do: If the eyes are affected, rinse the eyes and under the eyelids with water.
Note: Reworded from the page; no exact quote captured.
Do: Bitumen: very carefully try to crack the bitumen with scissors, taking care not to damage the skin underneath.
Quote: "Very carefully try to crack the bitumen with scissors taking care not to damage the skin underneath"
Say: The page does not say how long to soak a chemical burn. Follow the advice of the 111 call taker.
Note: Gap in the guideline: no soaking time for chemical burns, so this route does not use the 20-minute heat-burn timer.
Next: cover

### smoke: Burns in the mouth or throat
Warning: The throat, airway and lungs may be damaged. Call 111 urgently.
Note: Reworded from the page; no exact quote captured for this line.
Do: Move the person to a safe area, preferably outside into fresh air.
Quote: "Move the person to a safe area, preferably outside into fresh air."
Do: Call 111.
Say: If breathing is hard, help them into a position with the head and chest raised.
Note: Reworded from the page; no exact quote captured.
Note: Deliberate: the page's smoke section has no cooling or covering steps.
End:

### on-fire: Clothing on fire
Do: Smother the flames with a coat or blanket if you can.
Do: Stop, drop and roll: stop them running or waving their arms, get them to the ground and roll them to put the fire out.
Note: Both lines reworded from the page's "Clothing on fire" section; no exact quote captured.
Next: call-check

### cool: Cool the burn for at least 20 minutes
Do: Pour cool water over the burn.
Quote: "Pour cool water over the burn for at least 20 minutes."
Say: Any cool, clean liquid can be used. A hand or foot can go in a bowl or bucket of water.
Note: Reworded from the page; no exact quote captured.
Do: Remove tight clothing or jewellery near the burn (it may swell), and clothing splashed with hot liquid or chemicals.
Note: Reworded from the page; no exact quote captured.
Warning: Cool the burn, not the child. Do not put small children or babies into a cold bath or shower for a full 20 minutes.
Quote: "Put small children or babies into a cold bath or shower for a full 20 minutes"
Say: Use cool, not cold, water.
Quote: "Remember to cool the burn not the child, so use cool (NOT cold) water."
Note: The quote sits under the page's "DO NOT" heading; the app adds "Do not" so the meaning is kept (lessons trap 10).
Say: Don't break blisters, remove anything stuck to the burn, or put creams or lotions on it.
Note: From the page's "DO NOT" list, reworded; no exact quote captured.
Timer: 20 minutes -> cover
Say: The timer shows the minimum. The page says "at least" 20 minutes and gives no upper limit.
Note: Lessons trap 6: "at least 20 minutes" sets a minimum only. The page does not say what to do if 20 minutes of cooling is not possible.
Ask: Has the burn been cooled for at least 20 minutes?
- Yes -> cover
- It can't be cooled for 20 minutes -> cool-short

### cool-short: Cooling cut short
Warning: The page does not say what to do if the burn can't be cooled for 20 minutes.
Say: Call 111 for advice if you are unsure.
Note: Gap in the guideline (lessons trap 13). Advice to call 111 rests on the page's closing line.
Quote: "If you have a person in urgent need of medical attention, call 111 now."
Next: cover

### cover: Cover the burn
Do: Cover the burn with a non-adhesive dressing or loose cling film.
Quote: "Cover the burn by using a non-adhesive dressing or loose cling film."
Warning: Don't use cling film on the face.
Quote: "Don’t use cling film on the face."
Say: Use a sterile dressing if you have one, or wrap cling film or a plastic bag loosely around it. Don't use a sticky dressing: it can harm the skin around the burn.
Note: Reworded from the page; no exact quote captured.
Say: If you have a person in urgent need of medical attention, call 111 now.
Quote: "If you have a person in urgent need of medical attention, call 111 now."
End:

## Notes
Source copy: the St John site blocks direct download. Thirteen lines were captured word for word; every other line is reworded from a fetch of the page and is marked with a Note and has no quote.
Gap: the page gives no soaking time for chemical burns (that route skips the 20-minute timer) and no action if 20 minutes of cooling cannot be done (shown on screen).
Electrical burns: named as a cause, but the page gives no separate steps.
Gap: the page does not say when to see a doctor after first aid for heat burns (its sunburn section does); the app shows only its closing 111 line.
Sunburn is on the page but is left out of this app (not an emergency route).
