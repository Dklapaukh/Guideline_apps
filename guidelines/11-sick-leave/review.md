# Review: 11-sick-leave (NZ sick leave: entitlement, telling your employer, proof and who pays, pay, ACC)

Verdict: Faithful on numbers and careful about gaps. Every number on screen (10 days, 6 months, 10 hours, 1 hour / 40 hours, 20 days, 3 calendar days, 80%, 1 day per 5 days, 10 full days, 0800 20 90 20, 6 August 2028) is in source.txt. Nothing is invented. The 2028 law change is stated plainly on the start screen. Missing link words are not guessed. It needs changes before sign-off. The most serious problems: (1) the "who pays for proof" question asks how long you have been sick, but the pages decide it by how many days in a row you had been sick **when the employer asks**. The Holly and Jennifer lines that say this ("If ... had asked ... on Tuesday, her employer would have had to cover the cost") are left out, so a person off for 3+ days who was asked on day 1 or 2 is told "You pay". (2) The employer route's "Not sure how to count" leads to employee screens and then the employee pay screens. (3) The ACC screen says "The employer pays in the first week", which contradicts the next screen for injuries outside work. Also: 3 missed instructions, two contradictions between the pages that are not flagged (cash-up; "You must agree" for holidays), and `not-yet` mixes up new starters with later years.

## Findings

1. **Who pays for proof depends on when the employer asks, but the app asks "How long have you been sick?" (most serious, trap 6).**
   Where: `proof` question "How long have you been sick? (This decides who pays.)"; `proof-3plus` "You pay for it yourself."; `emp-proof`, `emp-3plus`; `proof-count` (Jennifer shown with only half her example).
   Source: A113-117 / B274-278 Jennifer: "Her employer can ask for proof on Friday at Jennifer's expense, as she has been sick for 4 days in a row. If Jennifer's employer had asked for proof when she was sick on Tuesday, her employer would have had to cover the cost." A118-125 / B279-286 Holly: "If Holly's employer had asked for the certificate when she phoned in sick on Tuesday, her employer would have had to cover the cost." ... "If Holly had come to work at lunchtime on Wednesday (and taken only a half day's sick leave), she would have been sick for less than 3 days." Also A108 "they can ask for proof on or after the third day."
   Problem: someone off sick all week who was asked for a certificate on day 2 will answer "3 or more days in a row" and be told "You pay for it yourself". The pages say the employer pays in that case. The Holly half-day case (a half day on day 3 counts as less than 3 days when the workplace records half-days) is also left out. Notes says the Holly example was dropped "to keep screens short", but it carries a rule, not just colour. The `proof-under3` warning "does not say what happens if your employer asks late" is then half wrong: the examples do say that asking on or after day 3 moves the cost to the employee. Counted as 1 missed instruction.
   Fix: change the `proof` and `emp-proof` question to "When your employer asked for proof, how many days in a row had you been sick (counting weekends and days off)?" (employer: "When you asked..."), with answers "Less than 3 days", "3 or more days in a row", "Not sure how to count". On `proof-3plus` and `emp-3plus` add `Say: If they asked before the third day, they pay, even if you were off longer.` and quote "If Jennifer's employer had asked for proof when she was sick on Tuesday, her employer would have had to cover the cost." On `proof-count` add the Holly half-day line, quoting "she would have been sick for less than 3 days." Reword the `proof-under3` warning to "Employment New Zealand does not say what happens if your employer asks late but you were back at work before the third day." Update Notes.

2. **Employer route: "Not sure how to count" leads into the employee screens and the employee pay route.**
   Where: `emp-proof` → "Not sure how to count" → `proof-count` → `proof-under3` / `proof-3plus` → `pay` → `pay-proof` (tested: the employer reads "your employer ... pay you back" and "If you don't give proof").
   Source: B263-273 (employer version: "you must: ask for proof as soon as possible, and pay the employee back"; "Your employee must pay for these costs.").
   Problem: the employer's voice and screens are flattened in one tap. The employer then misses `emp-pay` and `emp-pay-proof` (overtime, "If they provide proof at a later date, you must pay"). Notes admits this but it is easy to fix.
   Fix: add `emp-count` (the same calendar-days and Jennifer lines, quoting B271-277 in the employer voice: "You can ask for proof on Friday at Jennifer's expense") with answers to `emp-under3` / `emp-3plus`. Point `emp-proof` at it.

3. **ACC screen: "The employer pays in the first week" contradicts `acc-notwork`; "cannot make you take ACC time as sick leave" drops its condition (trap 10).**
   Where: `acc` first and second Say lines.
   Source: A145 "your employer has payment responsibilities for the first week of your injury." A150-152 "If your injury happened at work, your employer must pay you compensation of 80% ... If the injury did not happen at work, you can use sick leave or other paid leave ... If you do not have any paid leave available, you will not be paid for that first week." B309 "If the employee is receiving ACC payments, you cannot make them take this time as sick leave or annual holidays."
   Problem: for an injury outside work the employer does not pay the first week; the employee may use their own sick leave, or go unpaid. "Pays" turns "has payment responsibilities" into a promise the next screen takes back. "ACC time" drops "receiving ACC payments". In the first week of an injury outside work, the employee is told they *can* use sick leave, so the unconditional line reads as a contradiction.
   Fix: `Say: What happens in the first week depends on whether the injury happened at work. ACC pays from the second week.` (quote A145 and A146). `Say: While you are receiving ACC payments, your employer cannot make you take that time as sick leave or annual holidays.` (quote B309 in full, from "If the employee is receiving ACC payments").

4. **Missed instruction: no sick leave pay is owed for the same period as weekly ACC compensation.**
   Where: `acc` / `acc-topup`. Notes says it is left out.
   Source: C372 "If an employee is receiving weekly compensation from ACC (for example, after the first week), the employer does not have to pay them sick leave for the same period."
   Fix: add to `acc` after the line in finding 3: `Say: The employer does not have to pay sick leave for the same period as weekly ACC compensation (except as a top-up).` Quote C372 and C373 "Sick leave pay can be used to top-up ACC payments." Counted as 1 missed instruction.

5. **Missed instruction: on part-days, employers should check the employment agreement and leave policies. The pay choices are also missing.**
   Where: `part-day`.
   Source: C369 "When deciding how to apply sick, bereavement, or family violence leave entitlements for part-days, employers should check the [link words missing] and any related leave policies to see what they say." C363-367: with a half-day agreement, deduct a half day, "pay for the time worked, and pay half the employee's RDP ... for the rest of the day"; "Alternatively, employers can choose to pay ... a full day of RDP ... for a part-day of leave." B236 "You would then pay them half their [ ] or [ ] for the time on sick leave."
   Problem: the screen gives the deduction rules but not what is paid for a half day, and not the instruction to check the agreement. An employer reaching this (via `other`) cannot work out the pay. Counted as 1 missed instruction (C369). The pay choices are a smaller gap.
   Fix: add `Say: Check your employment agreement and leave policy for what they say about part-days.` (quote "and any related leave policies to see what they say."; the link word is missing, so don't name the document). Add `Say: With a half-day agreed: half a day off your sick leave, paid for the time worked plus half a day's pay for the rest.` Quote "pay for the time worked, and" and "for the rest of the day."

6. **Contradiction not flagged: cash-up of unused sick leave.**
   Where: `balance` Warning "Unused sick leave cannot be cashed up or paid out when you leave." Notes does not mention it.
   Source: A57 "Unused sick leave cannot be cashed up or be part of any final payment when you leave your job." B208 "Unused sick leave does not have to be cashed up and an employee is not entitled to have any unused sick leave paid out as part of any final payment".
   Problem: the employee page says "cannot"; the employer page says "does not have to" and "not entitled to", which suggests an employer may choose to. The app shows only the employee page's stronger word, and employers see it too (`balance` is reached from "Is my employee entitled?").
   Fix: change the warning to "You are not entitled to cash up unused sick leave or have it paid out when you leave." Add a line: "The employee page says it 'cannot' be cashed up; the employer page says it 'does not have to' be. Employment New Zealand does not say whether an employer may choose to." Add this to the Notes contradictions list.
