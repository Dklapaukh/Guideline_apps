# Sick leave (NZ)

About: Helps employees and employers in New Zealand with sick leave: who is entitled, telling your employer, proof of sickness and who pays for it, sick pay, running out, part-days, appointments and ACC. It follows the current rules in the Holidays Act; Employment New Zealand says these apply until the Employment Leave Act starts on 6 August 2028.

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch of five Employment New Zealand pages (Sections A to E of source.txt); small wording slips are possible. The sick leave landing page and "Changes to leave coming in 2028" could not be read. Saving the pages as PDFs into sources/ would make checking exact and recover the missing link words.
Law change: the Employment Leave Act 2026 has passed but starts on 6 August 2028, and "You cannot start following the new law early". The start screen says today's rules apply until then. No page says what will change for sick leave. The older Bill page (Section E, "expected to pass this year") is out of date and is not used.
Main route (sick today): today (tell your employer) -> proof (who can be covered, which doctor; who pays depends on how many days in a row the person had been sick WHEN the employer asked) -> proof-under3 / proof-3plus / proof-count -> pay -> pay-proof. Side routes: entitled (new-starter, not-yet, not-sure), none-left, holidays.
Voice: employee screens say "you"/"your employer"; employer screens (employer, emp-*) say "you"/"the employee", and the employer proof and pay route has its own screens (emp-proof, emp-count, emp-under3, emp-3plus, emp-pay, emp-pay-more, emp-pay-proof), quoting the employer page. Shared topic screens (entitled, balance, cashup, holidays, appointment, part-day, part-day-pay, stress, ongoing, acc*) use the employee voice; the employer reaches them through emp-other, which says they are written for employees and that differences from the employer page are shown on the screen (cash-up wording on cashup, "You must agree to this" on holidays). part-day and part-day-pay quote the pay and employer pages. Not split further, to keep the app small.
Gaps (said on screen): how, to whom and how far ahead to tell the employer (only "should ... as soon as possible", not "must"; nothing on what happens if you don't); what happens if the employer asks late but the person was back at work before day 3; how days that are not in a row count towards 3 days; how the employer comes to "know" about 3 days; what a "reasonable excuse" is; how long "a reasonable period of time" is; when the "12-month period" or sick-leave year starts; what a new starter gets before the 6-month rules are met (new-starter points to the no-sick-leave-left options without claiming they apply); whether an employer may choose to cash up sick leave. Not said on screen: "dependant" is never defined.
Contradictions and oddities (shown on screen, not resolved): (1) Whose sickness proof can cover: entitlement line "partners and children, or other dependants"; employee proof line "spouse, partner, child, or other dependant"; employer proof line "spouse, partner or dependant" (no child). Each route shows its own page's words. (2) "can accumulate up to 20 sick days a year" (both pages, part-time line) reads as a yearly amount; elsewhere 20 is the maximum balance carried over. The app uses the carry-over sentence only (Notes only). (3) No sick leave left: the employee page says the employer "does not have to agree ... unless your employment agreement says they do"; the employer page only says "you could agree". emp-none shows both. (4) Annual holidays: the employer page says "You must agree to this" for sick leave during holidays already started, which could mean the employer is required to agree; the employee page says "Your employer must agree to this" (agreement needed). holidays shows both. (5) Cash-up: employee page "cannot be cashed up"; employer page "does not have to be cashed up" and the employee "is not entitled to have" it paid out. cashup shows both and says the page does not say whether an employer may choose to.
Typos on the pages: "or or" and a stray ", or" bullet in the employee "no sick leave left" list; "counted as a using a whole day" on the pay page. Neither is quoted.
