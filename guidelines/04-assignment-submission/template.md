# Submitting a Law assignment online

About: Walks you through the checks before you submit a Law assignment, uploading it in Canvas with Turnitin, checking it arrived, and what the guidance says if something goes wrong.
Source: University of Auckland Faculty of Law, Assignment submission process - https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/law/current-students/llb-students/academic-information/assignment-submission-process.html
Source: University of Auckland Faculty of Law, Electronic Submission Instructions (PDF) - https://www.auckland.ac.nz/assets/law/Documents/2021/Undergraduate-courses/Academic-information/Electronic%20Submission%20Instructions%202021%20.pdf
Version: Faculty of Law "Assignment submission process" page and its "Electronic Submission Instructions" PDF (web address says 2021), both read 18 Nov 2026, no review date shown
Audience: University of Auckland Law (LLB) students submitting written work
Warning: This guidance is from the University of Auckland Faculty of Law, for Law (LLB) students. Your course's Canvas page has the full instructions for your course.
Reviewed by:

## Steps

### start: What do you need?
Say: This guidance is for Law (LLB) students at the University of Auckland. If you are not a Law student, follow your own course's instructions.
Say: Full instructions for each course are on that course's assignment page in Canvas.
Quote: "Full instructions on the assignment submission process for each course are shown on each assignment submission page in Canvas."
Ask: What do you need to do?
- Submit my assignment -> format
- Ask for more time (an extension) -> extension
- Something went wrong -> wrong

### format: How does your course want it?
Say: Most courses want a digital copy in Microsoft Word, by 12 noon on the due date.
Quote: "Written work for most courses must be submitted both in digital copy (Microsoft Word) by 12 noon on the due date, or late penalties will apply."
Say: Some courses want a hard copy and a digital copy.
Quote: "In some courses, assignments are submitted in hard copy and digital format."
Ask: How does your course ask for it?
- Online only, through Canvas -> online-only
- A hard copy and a digital copy -> hard-copy
- It's a take-home test -> take-home
- I'm not sure -> not-sure

### online-only: Online only
Say: Upload it on the Canvas assignment page. No hard copy is needed.
Quote: "The assignment must be uploaded via the Canvas assignment page."
Quote: "No hard copy submission is required."
Next: checks

### take-home: Take-home test
Say: For take-home tests, your course outline or Canvas tells you how to submit and what the late penalties are.
Quote: "You will be advised about applicable penalties and the format of submission of take-home tests in the course outlines and/or via Canvas."
Say: Late penalties for take-home tests depend on the length of the test.
Quote: "Lateness penalties for take-home tests vary depending on the length of the take-home test."
Next: checks

### hard-copy: Hard copy and digital copy
Say: The steps in this app are for the digital copy, uploaded in Canvas.
Warning: The guidance does not say how, where or by when to hand in a hard copy. Check your course outline or Canvas page, or ask your lecturer.
Next: checks

### not-sure: Not sure how to submit?
Say: Check your course's assignment page in Canvas. It has the full instructions for your course.
Quote: "Full instructions on the assignment submission process for each course are shown on each assignment submission page in Canvas."
Say: Get to know how your course wants it. Penalties apply if a submission is incomplete or incorrect.
Quote: "Students should familiarise themselves with the assignment submission process for each course, as penalties apply for incomplete/incorrect submissions."
Next: checks

### checks: Before you submit
Do: Take your name off the assignment. No student names.
Quote: "All assignments submitted must be anonymised, i.e. with NO STUDENT NAMES."
Do: Save it in Microsoft Word (.doc or .docx).
Quote: "The assignment must be submitted in Microsoft Word (.doc or .docx) format."
Do: Read the instructions on your course's assignment page in Canvas.
Quote: "Full instructions are available on the relevant course Canvas page."
Say: Penalties apply if a submission is incomplete or incorrect.
Quote: "Students should familiarise themselves with the assignment submission process for each course, as penalties apply for incomplete/incorrect submissions."
Say: The deadline is 12 noon on the due date. After that, late penalties apply.
Quote: "by 12 noon on the due date, or late penalties will apply."
Say: This guidance does not cover word limits, referencing or what to name your file. Check your course outline or ask your lecturer.
Next: open-assignment

### open-assignment: Open the assignment in Canvas
Do: Log in to Canvas (canvas.auckland.ac.nz).
Quote: "Log in to Canvas (canvas.auckland.ac.nz)."
Do: Choose your course.
Quote: "Select the Course."
Do: In the Course Navigation, click Assignments.
Quote: "In the Course Navigation, click the Assignments link."
Do: Click the name of the assignment.
Quote: "Click the name of the assignment."
Say: You will see the Turnitin dashboard.
Quote: "You will see the Turnitin dashboard below:"
Next: upload

### upload: Upload your file
Do: Click the Upload icon: the dark grey arrow pointing up.
Quote: "To submit your assignment, click the Upload icon (the dark grey arrow pointing up."
Do: In the new window, choose how to submit: a file upload, or a file from Google Drive or Dropbox.
Quote: "Select the submission type you want to use for your assignment."
Quote: "You can submit a file upload, or select a file from Google Drive or Dropbox."
Warning: Whichever you choose, the file must be Microsoft Word (.doc or .docx).
Quote: "The assignment must be submitted in Microsoft Word (.doc or .docx) format."
Say: Turnitin checks your file and uploads it. A window shows its progress.
Quote: "Turnitin validates your entry and prepares it for submission."
Quote: "Turnitin will then upload the document and show the following window as it goes through the process:"
Say: By uploading, you confirm your work follows the University's academic honesty policy.
Quote: "By uploading your assignment you affirm that your work complies fully with the University's academic honesty policy."
Next: accept

### accept: Check the preview
Say: Once the file has uploaded, you can see a preview of it.
Quote: "Once the file has been uploaded you can see a preview of the file you have uploaded."
Ask: Is it the right file?
- Yes -> accept-save
- No, it's the wrong file -> preview-wrong

### preview-wrong: The preview shows the wrong file
Warning: The guidance does not say what to do if the preview shows the wrong file. Contact your lecturer before the due date and time.
Quote: "Students experiencing problems with online submissions should contact their lecturer prior to the submission due date and time."
Ask: What next?
- Start again with the right file -> checks
- What the guidance says about resubmitting -> wrong-file
- Finish -> end

### accept-save: Accept your submission
Do: Click the blue button at the bottom: "Accept submission – save". You must click it.
Quote: "There will be a blue button at the bottom saying to "Accept submission – save"."
Quote: "You must click on that."
Next: confirm

### confirm: Check it arrived
Say: The window closes. The Turnitin dashboard now shows your document's title, and the date and time it was uploaded.
Quote: "The window will disappear and the Turnitin dashboard will now show information regarding the document you have uploaded including the title, and the date and time it was uploaded."
Do: Check the title is your assignment.
Quote: "the Turnitin dashboard will now show information regarding the document you have uploaded including the title, and the date and time it was uploaded."
Do: Check the date and time.
Quote: "the Turnitin dashboard will now show information regarding the document you have uploaded including the title, and the date and time it was uploaded."
Say: There are now three icons on the right. The last one (a page with lines) downloads your digital receipt.
Quote: "There are now three icons on the right."
Quote: "The very last icon on the right (paper/document with lines) allows you to download your digital receipt for your assignment (if you wish to do so; the Faculty does not require this)."
Do: Recommended: download your digital receipt and save a copy.
Quote: "You are recommended to download and save a copy of the digital receipt for your own records and to ensure online submission has been successful."
Say: It is not required, but you need it if you ever challenge a late penalty because of a technical problem.
Quote: "the Faculty does not require this"
Quote: "In order to do so a copy of the receipt of submission will be required."
Say: The middle icon (a down arrow) downloads the assignment you submitted, if you want to look at it.
Quote: "The second icon (down arrow) allows you to download the assignment you have submitted to Turnitin."
Ask: Did it work?
- Yes: I can see my assignment, with the date and time -> done
- No, something went wrong -> wrong

### done: All done
Say: Your digital copy is submitted. Keep your digital receipt somewhere safe.
Quote: "You are recommended to download and save a copy of the digital receipt for your own records and to ensure online submission has been successful."
Say: If you need to, the upload icon (the first icon) lets you submit again.
Quote: "The first is the upload icon (as from before) where you can resubmit if required."
Say: The guidance does not say whether a new upload replaces the old one. If you are unsure, ask your lecturer before the deadline.
Warning: If your course also wants a hard copy, you still need to hand that in. The guidance does not say how. Check your course outline or Canvas.
Quote: "In some courses, assignments are submitted in hard copy and digital format."
End:

### wrong: What went wrong?
Ask: What went wrong?
- It won't upload, or something isn't working -> technical
- I uploaded the wrong file -> wrong-file
- I have missed, or will miss, 12 noon -> late
- I need more time (an extension) -> extension

### technical: A technical problem
Do: Contact your lecturer before the due date and time.
Quote: "Students experiencing problems with online submissions should contact their lecturer prior to the submission due date and time."
Say: The guidance does not say what to do if the due time has already passed.
Say: If your upload ends up late because of a technical problem, you can only challenge the late penalty if you can prove a technical malfunction. You will need the receipt from your submission once it goes through, so download it then.
Quote: "You can only challenge a lateness penalty for late online submission if you can prove a technical malfunction."
Quote: "In order to do so a copy of the receipt of submission will be required."
Say: The guidance does not say how to prove a technical malfunction, and gives no help-desk contact. Ask your lecturer.
Ask: What next?
- Try uploading again -> checks
- Finish -> end

### wrong-file: Wrong file uploaded
Say: The upload icon (the first icon on the right) lets you submit again if you need to.
Quote: "The first is the upload icon (as from before) where you can resubmit if required."
Warning: The guidance does not say whether a new upload replaces the old one, or whether a new upload after 12 noon counts as late. Contact your lecturer before the due date and time.
Quote: "Students experiencing problems with online submissions should contact their lecturer prior to the submission due date and time."
Ask: What next?
- Upload the right file -> checks
- Finish -> end

### late: Late submission
Say: Take-home test? Its late penalties depend on its length. Check your course outline or Canvas.
Quote: "Lateness penalties for take-home tests vary depending on the length of the take-home test."
Quote: "You will be advised about applicable penalties and the format of submission of take-home tests in the course outlines and/or via Canvas."
Say: For online-only courses, the penalty is 5 marks for each day, or part of a day, that the digital copy is late.
Quote: "The penalty for lateness is 5 marks for each day (or part of day thereof) in which the digital copy of the assessment is submitted late."
Say: Days are calendar days, not 24-hour periods. Saturdays and Sundays count.
Quote: "For the avoidance of doubt, penalties are applied on a calendar day basis (not a 24-hour basis)."
Quote: "For the purposes of online-only submission, Saturdays and Sundays count as calendar days."
Say: The guidance gives no late penalties for courses that want a hard copy, and does not say if there is a last day after which late work is not accepted. Check your course outline or ask your lecturer.
Say: You can only challenge a late penalty if you can prove a technical malfunction, with a copy of your receipt.
Quote: "You can only challenge a lateness penalty for late online submission if you can prove a technical malfunction."
Quote: "In order to do so a copy of the receipt of submission will be required."
Ask: What next?
- Go to the upload steps -> checks
- I need an extension -> extension
- How is the penalty worked out? -> late-example
- Finish -> end

### late-example: How the late penalty is worked out
Say: The guidance's example: due Thursday at 12 noon.
Quote: "An assignment is due on Thursday 7 March at 12 noon:"
Say: Handed in at 2pm Thursday: 5 marks off.
Quote: "Student A hands in their copy at 2pm on Thursday 7 March."
Quote: "The lateness penalty applied will be a total of 5 marks deducted from the total available marks for the assignment:"
Say: Handed in at 5am Friday: 10 marks off (5 for Thursday, 5 for Friday).
Quote: "Student B hands in their copy at 5am on Friday 8 March."
Quote: "The lateness penalty applied will be a total of 10 marks deducted from the total available marks for the assignment:"
Next: late

### extension: Asking for an extension
Say: To apply for an extension, you must do both of these.
Quote: "To apply for an extension, you must:"
Do: Email lawextensions@auckland.ac.nz.
Quote: "email lawextensions@auckland.ac.nz"
Do: Send evidence, such as a doctor's certificate, before the due date.
Quote: "supply evidence (e.g. doctor’s certificate) before the due date."
Warning: Lecturers and tutors do not and cannot grant extensions.
Quote: "Lecturers and tutors do not and cannot grant extensions."
Say: The guidance says "before the due date" next to the evidence. It does not say whether the email must also be sent by then, or whether it means before the due day or before 12 noon on it.
Say: The guidance does not say what to do if the due date has already passed, or how long a reply takes. Check your course outline.
End:

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch of the Law page (Section A) and its linked PDF (Section B); the site blocks direct download, so small wording slips are possible. Saving both as PDFs into sources/ would make checking exact. Neither shows a date; the PDF's web address says 2021.
Audience: the guidance is the Faculty of Law's, for LLB students only; the start screen and banner say so.
Receipt (kept both visible): Section A "recommends" downloading and saving the digital receipt; Section B says "the Faculty does not require this". Section A also says the receipt is needed to challenge a late penalty. The confirm screen shows both and says they differ.
"both in digital copy (Microsoft Word)": the sentence says "both" but names only one copy (digital). It may have once named a hard copy too. The format screen shows the sentence as written and lets the student choose online only or hard copy and digital.
Hard copy: Section A says some courses want a hard copy and digital format, but gives no instructions for the hard copy (where, how, by when). The app says so and points to the course outline, Canvas or lecturer.
Penalties: the penalty, calendar-day and weekend rules are under the heading for online-only courses; the guidance does not give penalties for hard-copy courses. The Example 2 table (not quoted; described in brackets in source.txt) agrees with Example 1 (5 marks after noon on the due date, 10 on Day 1, up to 40 on Day 7), so the app shows only Example 1.
Gaps (shown on screen as "The guidance does not say ..."): word limits, referencing, file naming; whether a new upload before the deadline replaces the old one; what happens if you resubmit, or find a wrong file, after the deadline; how to prove a technical malfunction; any help-desk contact; a last day after which late work is not accepted; what to do about an extension if the due date has passed, and how long a reply takes; hard copy instructions.
Who to contact: technical problems go to the lecturer before the deadline (Section A); extensions go only to lawextensions@auckland.ac.nz, and "Lecturers and tutors do not and cannot grant extensions". Both are shown where they apply; they do not contradict each other.
Section B's Upload line has an unclosed bracket ("(the dark grey arrow pointing up."); the quote keeps it, the screen closes it.
Preview: "Make sure it is the right file" is the app's own question; the source only says "you can see a preview". The source does not say what to do if the preview shows the wrong file; the app says so and points to the lecturer.
Technical problems: the guidance does not say how to prove a malfunction when no submission went through, or what to do if the due time has already passed (it says to contact the lecturer "prior to the submission due date and time").
Extension timing: "before the due date" sits next to the evidence line only; the guidance does not say whether the email must also be before then, or whether "before the due date" means before the due day or before 12 noon on it. Shown on screen as a gap; no advice added.
Hard copy and done: conditions can only test inputs, not the route, so the done screen always carries a line that a hard copy, if needed, must still be handed in.
Take-home tests: the format pointer and penalty lines are on their own screen from the format question, and first on the late screen.
No timer or reminder: the only time is a clock deadline (12 noon on the due date), which the app states.
Not on screen: the Turnitin originality policy link (background).
