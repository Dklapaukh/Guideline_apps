# Earthquake: Drop, Cover and Hold, then what to do after

About: Shows what to do while the ground is shaking, wherever you are, and then what to do when the shaking stops.
Source: Get Ready (NEMA), Earthquakes - https://getready.govt.nz/emergency/earthquakes/
Source: Get Ready (NEMA), Earthquakes, NZ Sign Language version - https://nzsl.getready.govt.nz/emergency/earthquakes
Version: Get Ready (NEMA) earthquakes page and its NZ Sign Language version (nzsl.getready.govt.nz/emergency/earthquakes), read 18 Nov 2026, no review date shown
Audience: Anyone in New Zealand during or just after an earthquake
Warning: Shaking now? Drop, Cover and Hold first. Read this when you are safe.
Reviewed by:

## Steps

### start: Shaking now: Drop, Cover and Hold
Do: Drop, Cover and Hold until the shaking is over.
Quote: "Drop, Cover and Hold until the shaking is over."
Warning: Do not run outside. You could be hit by falling bricks and glass.
Quote: "Do not run outside or you risk getting hit by falling bricks and glass."
Say: Use the version that works for your body.
Quote: "The best version of Drop, Cover, Hold is the one that works for your body and that you have practiced."
Ask: Where are you?
- Inside a building -> indoors
- In bed -> in-bed
- I have a mobility problem, or use a cane, walker or wheelchair -> mobility
- Outside -> outside
- Driving -> driving
- In a lift -> lift
- Somewhere else -> elsewhere

### mobility: Your version of Drop, Cover and Hold
Say: Use the version that works for your body.
Quote: "The best version of Drop, Cover, Hold is the one that works for your body and that you have practiced."
Ask: Which is closest to you?
- I have a mobility impairment or use a cane -> cane
- I can't get up from the floor without help -> cane
- I use a walker or wheelchair -> wheelchair

### indoors: Inside: Drop, Cover and Hold
Warning: If you can't get up from the floor without help, don't drop to the ground.
Quote: "If you can’t get up from the floor without help, don’t drop to the ground in an earthquake."
Do: Drop down on your hands and knees, or as low as you can.
Quote: "Drop down on your hands and knees or as low as you can."
Say: If you can't drop to the floor, brace yourself against a wall or sturdy furniture.
Quote: "Brace yourself against a wall or sturdy furniture if you cannot drop to the floor."
Do: Cover your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk, if one is a few steps away.
Quote: "COVER your head and neck (or your entire body if possible) under a sturdy table or desk (if it is within a few steps of you)."
Say: No shelter nearby? Cover your head and neck with your arms and hands.
Quote: "If there is no shelter nearby, cover your head and neck with your arms and hands."
Do: Hold on to your shelter, or your position, until the shaking stops.
Quote: "HOLD on to your shelter (or your position to protect your head and neck) until the shaking stops."
Say: Under a table, hold on to the table legs. If the shaking moves your shelter, move with it.
Quote: "Hold on to the table legs to keep it from moving away from you."
Quote: "If the shaking shifts your shelter around, move with it."
Say: Keep away from windows and tall furniture. Not in a doorway.
Quote: "Away from windows that can shatter and cause injury."
Quote: "And away from tall furniture that can fall on you."
Quote: "Not in a doorway."
Say: In most homes a doorway is no stronger than the rest of the house, and a swinging door can cause more injury.
Quote: "In most homes, doorways are not stronger than any other part of a house and a swinging door can cause more injury."
Note: The table, no-shelter, hold and move-with-it lines are only on the Sign Language page (Section B). The windows, tall furniture and doorway lines come from its "Get ready" list of safe spaces.
Next: shaking-stops

### in-bed: In bed: stay in bed
Do: Stay in bed. Pull the sheets and blankets over you.
Quote: "If you are in bed, stay in bed and pull the sheets and blankets over you and use your pillow to protect your head and neck."
Do: Use your pillow to protect your head and neck.
Quote: "If you are in bed, stay in bed and pull the sheets and blankets over you and use your pillow to protect your head and neck."
Note: Only on the Sign Language page (Section B).
Next: shaking-stops

### cane: Using a cane, or can't get up from the floor
Warning: If you can't get up from the floor without help, don't drop to the ground.
Quote: "If you can’t get up from the floor without help, don’t drop to the ground in an earthquake."
Do: Get as low as you can, or sit in a chair, on the side of the bed or on a couch.
Quote: "If you have a mobility impairment or use a cane, get as low as you can or sit on in a chair, on the side of the bed or on a couch."
Do: Cover your head and neck with both hands.
Quote: "Cover your head and neck with both hands."
Do: Curl your body as much as you can into your lap.
Quote: "Curl your body as much as you can into your lap."
Say: Keep your cane near you so you can use it when the shaking stops.
Quote: "Keep your cane near you so you can use it when the shaking stops."
Note: The cane lines are only on the Sign Language page (Section B). Its "sit on in a chair" is a typo on the page; the quote keeps it as written and the screen says "sit in a chair".
Next: shaking-stops

### wheelchair: Walker or wheelchair: Lock, Cover and Hold
Do: Lock your wheels and get as low as you can.
Quote: "Lock your wheels and get as low as you can."
Do: Cover your head and neck with both hands if you can.
Quote: "Cover your head and neck with both hands if you can."
Do: Curl your body as much as you can into your lap.
Quote: "Curl your body as much as you can into your lap."
Do: Hold on until the shaking stops.
Quote: "Hold on until the shaking stops."
Note: Only on the Sign Language page (Section B): "If you use a walker or wheelchair, Lock, Cover and Hold."
Next: shaking-stops

### outside: Outside
Do: If you are outside, move no more than a few steps away from buildings, trees, streetlights and power lines, then Drop, Cover and Hold.
Quote: "If you are outside, move no more than a few steps away from buildings, trees, streetlights and power lines, then Drop, Cover and Hold."
Say: The guideline's wording here is unclear: it does not plainly say how far to move, or which way. Follow local advice.
Do: Drop down on your hands and knees, or as low as you can.
Quote: "Drop down on your hands and knees or as low as you can."
Do: Cover your head and neck if you can.
Quote: "Cover your head and neck if you can."
Do: Hold on to your position or shelter.
Quote: "Hold on to your position or shelter."
Say: The guideline does not give advice for crowds, or for being near cliffs or steep slopes. Follow local advice.
Note: Only on the Sign Language page (Section B). The screen shows the outside sentence word for word because it is ambiguous (it can be read as "stay within a few steps of buildings"); the app does not choose a reading. Gap: nothing on cliffs, slopes or crowds outdoors. The Drop, Cover and Hold how-to lines are from Section A.
Next: shaking-stops

### driving: Driving
Do: Pull over to a clear place and stop.
Quote: "If you are driving, pull over to a clear location."
Quote: "Stop."
Do: Wait there with your seatbelt on until the shaking stops.
Quote: "Wait there with your seatbelt fastened until the shaking stops."
Say: When the shaking stops, drive with caution. Avoid bridges and ramps: they may be damaged.
Quote: "Once the shaking stops, proceed with caution and avoid bridges or ramps as they may have been damaged."
Note: Only on the Sign Language page (Section B).
Next: shaking-stops

### lift: In a lift
Say: If you are in a lift, Drop, Cover and Hold.
Quote: "If you are in an elevator, Drop, Cover and Hold."
Do: Drop down on your hands and knees, or as low as you can.
Quote: "Drop down on your hands and knees or as low as you can."
Do: Cover your head and neck if you can.
Quote: "Cover your head and neck if you can."
Do: Hold on to your position or shelter.
Quote: "Hold on to your position or shelter."
Say: When the shaking stops, get out at the nearest floor if you can do it safely.
Quote: "When the shaking stops, try and get out at the nearest floor if you can safely do so."
Say: The guideline does not say what to do if the lift is stuck. Follow local advice.
Note: Only on the Sign Language page (Section B). Gap: nothing on a stuck lift.
Next: shaking-stops

### elsewhere: Somewhere else
Say: In an earthquake, Drop, Cover and Hold.
Quote: "In an earthquake Drop, Cover and Hold."
Do: Drop down on your hands and knees, or as low as you can.
Quote: "Drop down on your hands and knees or as low as you can."
Do: Cover your head and neck if you can.
Quote: "Cover your head and neck if you can."
Do: Hold on to your position or shelter.
Quote: "Hold on to your position or shelter."
Say: The guideline does not give separate advice for crowds, a moving bus or train, being near a cliff or slope, or holding a baby or child. Follow local advice.
Say: When the shaking stops, if you are in a shop, an unfamiliar building or on public transport, follow the instructions of those in charge.
Quote: "If you are in a store, unfamiliar commercial building or on public transport, follow the instructions of those in charge."
Note: The "those in charge" line is in the page's "after an earthquake" list, so the screen starts it with "When the shaking stops"; it is the only line on public transport.
Next: shaking-stops

### shaking-stops: When the shaking stops: near the coast?
Say: If you are near the coast, remember: Long or Strong, Get Gone.
Quote: "If you are near the coast remember, Long or Strong, Get Gone."
Ask: Are you near the coast?
- Yes -> long-strong
- No -> after

### long-strong: Long or Strong, Get Gone
Say: Long: the earthquake lasted longer than a minute. Strong: it was hard to stand.
Quote: "If the earthquake lasts longer than a minute or is strong enough to make it difficult to stand, move quickly to the nearest high ground or as far inland as you can out of tsunami evacuation zones."
Say: The guideline does not say what to do if you are not sure. Follow local advice.
Ask: Was it long or strong?
- Yes: longer than a minute, or hard to stand -> get-gone
- No -> after

### get-gone: Get Gone now
Warning: Move quickly to the nearest high ground, or as far inland as you can, out of tsunami evacuation zones.
Quote: "If the earthquake lasts longer than a minute or is strong enough to make it difficult to stand, move quickly to the nearest high ground or as far inland as you can out of tsunami evacuation zones."
Say: The guideline does not say whether to walk or drive. Follow local advice.
Say: Expect more shaking. Each time you feel shaking, Drop, Cover and Hold.
Quote: "Expect more shaking."
Quote: "Each time you feel earthquake shaking, Drop, Cover and Hold."
Say: Find out more about how to protect yourself from a tsunami on the Get Ready website.
Quote: "Find out more about how to protect yourself from a tsunami."
Note: This route ends here on purpose: the after-earthquake list ("Do not run outside", gas, neighbours, insurance) must not delay or contradict leaving a tsunami zone, and the source puts no job before evacuating.
End:

### after: Check yourself
Say: You do not have to leave a building straight away, unless it shows obvious signs of damage or you are in a tsunami evacuation zone.
Quote: "You do not have to evacuate a building straight away unless it is showing obvious signs of distress or you are in a tsunami evacuation zone."
Say: The guideline says "signs of distress" and does not say what these signs are.
Note: "signs of distress" is reworded as "signs of damage" on screen (plainer); the quote keeps the source words.
Warning: Expect more shaking. Each time you feel shaking, Drop, Cover and Hold.
Quote: "Expect more shaking."
Quote: "Each time you feel earthquake shaking, Drop, Cover and Hold."
Do: Check yourself for injuries. Get first aid if you need it.
Quote: "Check yourself for injuries and get first aid if necessary."
Say: Do not run outside. Staying inside is frightening, but it is much safer than going outside.
Quote: "Do not run outside."
Quote: "It is frightening to stay in a building immediately after an earthquake, but it is much safer than going outside."
Do: If you can, put on clothes that cover your arms and legs, and sturdy shoes.
Quote: "If you can, put on protective clothing that covers your arms and legs, and sturdy footwear."
Say: This protects you from broken objects.
Quote: "This is to protect yourself from injury by broken objects."
Next: hazards

### hazards: Gas, power and water
Say: Turn off water, electricity and gas if you are advised to.
Quote: "Turn off water, electricity and gas if advised to."
Warning: If you smell gas or hear a blowing or hissing noise: open a window, get everyone out quickly, and turn off the gas if you can.
Quote: "If you smell gas or hear a blowing or hissing noise, open a window, get everyone out quickly and turn off the gas if you can."
Warning: If you see sparks, broken wires or other electrical damage: turn off the electricity at the main fuse box, if it is safe to do so.
Quote: "If you see sparks, broken wires or evidence of electrical system damage, turn off the electricity at the main fuse box if it is safe to do so."
Next: others

### others: Others around you
Do: Check on your neighbours and anyone who might need your help.
Quote: "Check on your neighbours and anyone who might need your help."
Say: In a shop, an unfamiliar building or on public transport, follow the instructions of those in charge.
Quote: "If you are in a store, unfamiliar commercial building or on public transport, follow the instructions of those in charge."
Say: Use social media or text messages instead of calling, to keep phone lines clear for emergency calls.
Quote: "Use social media or text messages instead of calling to keep phone lines clear for emergency calls."
Do: Keep control of your pets. Protect them from hazards, and protect other people from your animals.
Quote: "Keep control of your pets."
Quote: "Protect them from hazards and protect other people from your animals."
Ask: Is your home or property damaged?
- Yes -> property
- No -> finish

### property: If your property is damaged
Warning: Do not do anything that puts your safety at risk or causes more damage to your property.
Quote: "Do not do anything that puts your safety at risk or causes more damage to your property."
Say: The guideline does not say when it is safe to go back into a damaged building. Follow local advice.
Do: Contact your insurance company as soon as possible.
Quote: "Contact your insurance company as soon as possible."
Do: If you rent, contact your landlord and your contents insurance company.
Quote: "If you rent your property, contact your landlord and your contents insurance company."
Do: Take photos of any damage. It will help speed up your claims.
Quote: "Take photos of any damage."
Quote: "It will help speed up assessments of your claims."
Next: finish

### finish: Keep safe
Warning: Expect more shaking. It can happen minutes, days, weeks, months and even years after an earthquake.
Quote: "More shaking can happen minutes, days, weeks, months and even years following an earthquake."
Say: Each time you feel shaking, Drop, Cover and Hold.
Quote: "Each time you feel earthquake shaking, Drop, Cover and Hold."
Say: Find your local Civil Defence Emergency Management (CDEM) Group, and find out what support you can get if you have been affected.
Quote: "Find your local Civil Defence Emergency Management (CDEM) Group."
Quote: "Find out what support you can get if you have been affected by an emergency event."
Say: First aid saves lives. The St John website shows what to look for and what to do.
Quote: "First aid saves lives."
End:

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch of both pages (the site blocks direct download), so small wording slips are possible; saving both pages as PDFs into sources/ would make checking exact. Neither page shows a date.
Two pages: Section A (main page) has the short Drop, Cover and Hold steps and the after-earthquake list. The situation lines (table, no shelter, cane, walker or wheelchair, bed, outside, lift, driving) and the safe-space list appear only on the Sign Language version (Section B); the main page has dropped them. Where both pages have a line, the app uses the main page wording.
Wording differences: Section A says "Do not run outside or you risk getting hit by falling bricks and glass", Section B adds "in an earthquake". Section A says "Drop down", "Cover your head and neck if you can" and "Hold on to your position or shelter"; Section B has longer COVER and HOLD lines (under a table, or arms and hands). The indoors step uses the longer Section B lines because they say what to do with and without shelter. Section A uses curly apostrophes ("can’t"), Section B straight ones. The gas line ("If you smell gas or hear a blowing or hissing noise") is from Section A; Section B has no after-earthquake list, and the researcher flagged this wording as differing between versions of the page. The app uses Section A.
Typo kept: the cane line reads "sit on in a chair" on the page; the quote keeps it, the screen says "sit in a chair".
Gaps (shown on screen as "The guideline does not say ...; follow local advice"): crowds, a moving bus or train, cliffs or steep slopes, babies or children, a stuck lift, what to do if you are not sure the earthquake was long or strong, whether to walk or drive when you Get Gone, and when it is safe to go back into a damaged building.
Not on screen: the "Get ready before" advice (practise twice a year, ShakeOut, identify safe spaces) is preparation, not a step during or after an earthquake; only the safe-space lines are used, in the indoors step. Section A's reasons for Drop, Cover and Hold are background.
Long or Strong: the page gives "longer than a minute" as the test; it is shown as text, with no timer, because the person is taking cover while it is shaking and the page gives no instruction to time it.
Get Gone route: it ends at the Get Gone screen, so a person leaving a tsunami zone never sees "Do not run outside" or the after-earthquake jobs. The source does not say what to do after reaching high ground; that is a gap.
Outside line: the source sentence "move no more than a few steps away from buildings, trees, streetlights and power lines" is ambiguous (it can be read as staying near buildings). The screen shows it word for word, says the wording is unclear, and does not choose a reading; the owner or a reviewer should decide.
Plain words: "obvious signs of distress" (of a building) is shown as "obvious signs of damage" on screen, with a line saying the guideline does not define the signs; the quote keeps the source words.
Review round 1 (review.md): fixed 1 (Get Gone ends; the evacuate-unless line is first on the after screen), 2 (shown word for word with an "unclear" line, not split, so no reading is chosen), 3, 4, 5 ("When the shaking stops" added; kept on Somewhere else because it is the only public transport line), 6 (7 answers: mobility grouped behind one answer with a short follow-up; "Somewhere else" shortened), 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 (clothing and doorway reasons added; "not like a fire" left as background). 12 left as is (preparation advice is out of scope by design).
