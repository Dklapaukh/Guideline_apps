# More time for an assessment (AUT)

About: Helps you work out whether to ask for a 3-day short extension or apply for special consideration at AUT, when to apply, what evidence you need and by when, and where to get help.
Source: Auckland University of Technology (AUT), Special consideration for an assessment - https://www.aut.ac.nz/student-life/studying/study-help/assessments-and-exams/special-consideration
Version: AUT "Special consideration for an assessment" web page, no date or review date shown, read 19 Nov 2026
Audience: AUT students who need more time for an assignment, or whose test or exam has been affected by something serious
Warning: This is AUT's guidance, for AUT students. If you study somewhere else, follow your own university's rules.
Reviewed by:

## Steps

### start: What do you need?
Say: If something serious and outside your control affects an assessment, you can apply for special consideration. You could also ask for a short extension of up to 3 days.
Quote: "your ability to attempt or prepare for an assessment may be seriously affected by exceptional circumstances beyond your control."
Quote: "If this happens, you can apply for special consideration for that assessment."
Quote: "You could also apply for a short extension of up to 3 days"
Ask: What do you need?
- Just a couple of extra days -> short-check
- Something serious is affecting me -> reason
- My due date (or test or exam) has already passed -> past-due
- I've applied. What happens next? -> next-steps

### short-check: Can you get a short extension?
Say: A short extension gives you 3 calendar days. You don't need any evidence.
Quote: "The short extension is for 3 calendar days"
Quote: "You don't need to provide evidence for a short extension."
Say: You can get one only if all three of these are true:
Say: 1. It's an assignment, not a test or exam. Nothing done under controlled conditions can have a short extension.
Quote: "You can't apply for short extensions for tests and exams; any assessment conducted under controlled conditions is not eligible for a short extension"
Say: 2. You haven't had a short extension in this course yet. You get one per course.
Quote: "You can only have one short extension per course."
Say: 3. You apply by midnight the day BEFORE it's due. For example, if it's due at 4pm on Monday, apply before midnight on Sunday.
Quote: "You must apply by midnight the day BEFORE your assessment is due"
Quote: "For example, if an assessment is due at 4pm on Monday 20 May, in order to be eligible for a short extension, you must apply BEFORE midnight on Sunday 19 May."
Ask: Are all three true for you?
- Yes, all three -> short-apply
- No, at least one isn't -> short-no

### short-apply: Apply for your short extension
Do: Open AUT's Special Consideration Application (SCA) tool. The link is on AUT's "Special consideration for an assessment" page.
Quote: "You must apply using the Special Consideration Application tool"
Do: The first time, Microsoft asks you to approve access. Click 'accept' and carry on.
Quote: "When you're accessing the tool for the first time, you'll be asked by Microsoft to approve access to the new software."
Quote: "Make sure you click on ‘accept’ and proceed."
Do: On the second screen, choose 'short extension'.
Quote: "select ‘short extension’ on the second screen"
Say: Need more than 3 days because of something serious? Then you need a standard extension, with evidence.
Quote: "If you need more time than 3 days because of valid exceptional circumstances, you need to apply for the standard extension and provide evidence"
Say: The page doesn't say how you'll hear that your short extension is confirmed, or what your new due time is. If you're unsure, contact the Student Hub: studenthub@aut.ac.nz or 0800 288 864.
Quote: "Email: studenthub@aut.ac.nz"
Quote: "Phone: 0800 288 864"
Ask: Is that everything?
- Yes, I've applied -> end
- I need more than 3 days -> reason

### short-no: A short extension isn't possible
Say: If something serious is affecting you, you can apply for special consideration instead. You'll need to provide evidence.
Quote: "If this happens, you can apply for special consideration for that assessment."
Quote: "If you need more time than 3 days because of valid exceptional circumstances, you need to apply for the standard extension and provide evidence"
Quote: "If you need further extensions for the same course, you'll then need to provide evidence of exceptional circumstances."
Say: Missed the short-extension deadline? You can still apply for special consideration, up to 5 working days after the due date.
Quote: "You can submit an SCA up to 5 working days after your assessment due date."
Say: The page offers nothing else if you simply need more time. If you're unsure, contact the Student Hub: studenthub@aut.ac.nz or 0800 288 864.
Quote: "Email: studenthub@aut.ac.nz"
Quote: "Phone: 0800 288 864"
Ask: Is something serious affecting you?
- Yes -> reason
- No -> end

### reason: What's affecting you?
Say: Special consideration is for when something serious, beyond your control, affects an assessment. Pick the closest match.
Quote: "seriously affected by exceptional circumstances beyond your control"
Ask: What's affecting you?
- A medical reason (illness, injury, COVID-19) -> report-medical
- My emotional or mental wellbeing -> report-mental
- Bereavement, being a victim of crime, or something else -> other-reason
- A sporting, cultural, religious or AUT commitment -> commitment
- Computer or printer trouble, lost work, or I misread the date -> not-eligible

### report-medical: You need a Student Impairment Report
Say: For a medical reason, send a Student Impairment Report with your application. A suitably qualified person fills it in.
Quote: "you need to provide a completed Student Impairment Report to accompany your Special Consideration Application form."
Quote: "The Student Impairment Report must be completed by a suitably qualified person."
Say: COVID-19 counts: if you're unwell or isolating, have ongoing effects, or your whānau or household has been seriously affected. Choose the 'Medical' reason.
Quote: "You can apply for a special consideration for assessment if you have COVID-19 and are unwell or you’re needing to isolate."
Quote: "You can also apply if you're suffering from ongoing health effects due to COVID-19 or your whānau or household has been seriously affected by COVID-19."
Quote: "Use the ‘Medical’ reason for application."
Say: If you couldn't see anyone in time through no fault of your own, that can be taken into account.
Quote: "though delays in accessing medical care through no fault of your own can be taken into account"
Ask: Who will fill in your report?

- My own doctor or practitioner outside AUT -> sir-external

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch of AUT's "Special consideration for an assessment" page (the site blocks direct download); eleven key lines were confirmed exact in a second read, but small wording slips are possible. Saving the page as a PDF into sources/ would make checking exact. No date or review date on the page.
Audience: AUT's page is university-wide and covers assignments and tests/exams together under "assessment". The banner says it is AUT's guidance and others should follow their own university (not repeated on the start screen). Screens say "due date (or test or exam date)" where timing matters, since the failing-grade line covers "assignment or examination".
Links not captured: the SCA tool link, the Student Impairment Report download, and the Student Medical Centre and AUTSA links. The app points students to AUT's page instead of inventing addresses. Student Hub email and phone are on the page and quoted.
Two routes through the reasons: conditions can only test inputs, not the route, so a student who says their due date has passed goes through their own copies of the reason screens (ids ending "-after"). These lead straight to the after-due-date apply screen, so the student is never asked again whether it is before or after the due date, and never gets the before-due-date evidence clock (lessons trap 6). The "-after" not-eligible screen does not offer a short extension (no longer possible). Keep the two copies the same when editing.
Short extension length: the page says "up to 3 days" (intro), "a short extension (3 days)" and "3 calendar days". The app uses "3 calendar days", the most specific (lessons trap 5); "up to" is shown on the start screen as printed.
Short extension deadline: "by midnight the day BEFORE" and the example "BEFORE midnight on Sunday 19 May" for a Monday 4pm due date. The app gives the example without the dates (20 May / 19 May) so it reads for any year; the quote keeps them.
Short extension checklist: the three eligibility rules (not a test/exam/controlled conditions, one per course, by midnight the day before) are on one screen with one yes/no question, to keep decisions few. The page does not say how a student is told the short extension is granted or what the new due time is (said on screen).
"Standard extension": used once on the page ("apply for the standard extension and provide evidence"); everywhere else it says special consideration / SCA. The page does not say whether these are the same thing; the app sends the student to the special consideration route (the SCA tool) and does not claim they are identical.
Commitments: applications for sporting, cultural, religious and AUT-related commitments "must be made before the date of the commitment". The app states this as a rule and asks whether the commitment date has passed. If it has, the app says plainly that the page requires applying beforehand, that it doesn't say whether a late application can be considered, and points to the Student Hub and AUTSA. If not, the student carries on to apply (the past-due copy goes straight to the after-due-date apply screen).
Not eligible: misreading dates, computer/printer problems (unless an officially notified failure of university equipment), losing data. The app offers the short extension (not on the past-due route) and sends an equipment-failure case to the "What to send" screen, which carries the "depends on the situation, explained in the SCA form" line.
Evidence timing (lessons trap 6): before/on the due date -> 5 working days from submitting the application; after the due date -> 5 working days from the original due date. Shown where the student applies, and again on the "What happens next" screen. The page does not define working days (public holidays, university closedowns); the "I'm not sure" screen says so and points to the Student Hub.
Student Impairment Report timing (lessons traps 5, 6, 13): the page says the report must "accompany your Special Consideration Application form", and separately that supporting evidence is due within 5 working days. It does not say whether the report can be uploaded later within that window. Both apply screens show both readings and point to the Student Hub; the app does not pick one.
Failing grade and notifying the teaching department: the page puts the failing-grade line under "If you apply AFTER the due date"; the app shows it on the after-due-date apply screen only, as a Do: to tell the teaching department, because the page makes notifying the department a condition. "Within the required time" and how to notify are not defined (said on screen).
Past due: more than 5 working days -> the page gives no route; the app says so and points to the Student Hub and AUTSA. "I'm not sure" -> its own screen (working days not defined; contact the Student Hub now; option to carry on and apply).
Medical/mental health: a Student Impairment Report from a suitably qualified person must accompany the application. Medical: AUT Student Medical Centre registered nurses, or an external practitioner using the downloaded form. Emotional/mental: Student Hub advisor (booking path quoted), or an external practitioner. Each route asks one question ("Who will fill in your report?") and then shows a short screen for that choice. On the mental-health screen the counselling line is first, so urgent support isn't at the bottom; no crisis phone numbers are added because the page gives none. COVID-19 uses the 'Medical' reason. "A medical reason (illness, injury, COVID-19)" is plain-English for the page's "Medical ... conditions". The page does not say what evidence is needed for bereavement, crime or other reasons ("explained in the SCA form").
Gaps (shown on screen): who decides, how long a decision takes, possible outcomes, any formal appeal (the only route is AUTSA advocates if the outcome feels unfair), what happens after 5 working days, whether a late commitment application can be considered, how a short extension is confirmed, how and when to notify the teaching department, whether the Student Impairment Report can follow the application.
No timer or reminder: the deadlines are calendar/working-day dates the app can't know, so it states them instead.
Not on screen: "Medical or other evidence needs to be provided" is covered by the report screens; the "Permissions request" heading is folded into the apply steps.
