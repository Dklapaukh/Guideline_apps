# Severe bleeding

About: Guides you through first aid in New Zealand when someone is bleeding badly: pressing on the wound, calling 111, a tourniquet on an arm or leg, objects stuck in a wound, a cut-off body part, bleeding inside, and a nosebleed. It keeps the times the ambulance will need.
Source: ANZCOR Guideline 9.1.1, First Aid for Management of Bleeding (April 2021, editorial update July 2025) (main source) - https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-1-1-first-aid-for-management-of-bleeding
Source: Hato Hone St John NZ, "Bleeding" - https://www.stjohn.org.nz/First-Aid/First-Aid-Library/Bleeding/
Source: Healthify He Puna Waiora, "Bleeding first aid" (reviewed 9 June 2026) - https://healthify.nz/health-a-z/b/bleeding
Version: ANZCOR Guideline 9.1.1 'First Aid for Management of Bleeding' (Approved April 2021, supersedes July 2017; Editorial Update July 2025 on tourniquets); Hato Hone St John 'Bleeding' first aid library page (no date shown, footer 2025); Healthify He Puna Waiora 'Bleeding first aid' (Last reviewed 09 Jun 2026); all read 20 Nov 2026
Audience: Anyone helping a person who is bleeding badly in New Zealand
Warning: Bleeding badly? Call 111. The sources do not all agree; where they differ, the app shows each one.
Reviewed by:

## Steps

### start: Bleeding badly? Press and call 111
Do: Press hard on the wound with a clean cloth, or your hand. Keep the edges of the wound together. Keep pressing.
Quote: "Firm pressure on or around the wound is the most effective way to stop most bleeding." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "If there isn't a cloth nearby, don't waste time – use the person's hand or your hand (wear gloves) to press on the wound and keep the edges of the wound together." (St John)
Record: Pressure started
Do: Call 111, or get someone else to call. Lie the person down.
Quote: "Lie the person down, apply pressure and call for an ambulance." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "get somebody else to call 111 for you" (Healthify)
Record: 111 called
Warning: Something stuck in the wound? Do not pull it out. Press around it.
Quote: "Do not remove the embedded object because it may be plugging the wound and restricting bleeding." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "use pressure around the object" (ANZCOR)
Say: Awake? Check they are happy for you to help. Not awake? Don't delay.
Quote: "If the person is conscious, check they are happy for you to touch them before you give first aid." (St John)
Quote: "If the person is unconscious and you need to check for injuries or give lifesaving first aid, then don't delay." (St John)
Say: Gloves (and glasses) if you have them, or a plastic bag over your hands. Don't wait for them.
Quote: "Use standard precautions (e.g. gloves, protective glasses) if readily available." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "If there are no gloves, place your hands inside a plastic bag." (St John)
Ask: What do you see?
- Pouring, spurting or soaking through -> soaking
- A body part cut off -> amputation
- Pressing, bleeding is slowing -> care
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Other: object stuck, nose, inside, head -> other

### soaking: Still bleeding? Press harder
Do: Keep pressing. Add a second pad and a tighter bandage over the wound.
Quote: "If bleeding continues, apply a second pad and a tighter bandage over the wound." (ANZCOR)
Record: Second pad added
Do: Still bleeding? Check the pad is right over the bleeding point. If not, take it off and place it again.
Quote: "check that the pad and bandage are correctly applied, directly over the bleeding." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "it may be necessary to remove the pad(s) to ensure that a specific bleeding point has not been missed." (ANZCOR)
Say: Pressing hard on 1 or 2 pads works better than piling on more (ANZCOR).
Quote: "only using 1 to 2 pads over a small area, will achieve greater pressure over the bleeding point than continuing to layer up further pads." (ANZCOR)
Say: The others differ:
- Healthify: add more padding; don't keep lifting it to check.
Quote: "Keep pressure on and add more padding if needed." (Healthify)
Quote: "don't keep lifting the dressing to check." (Healthify)
- St John: keep pressing firmly until the ambulance arrives.
Quote: "continue to press firmly until the ambulance arrives." (St John)
Ask: Still bleeding badly?
- Yes, from an arm or leg -> tourniquet
- Yes, from somewhere else (neck, body) -> dressing
- No, it is under control -> care
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing

### tourniquet: Arm or leg: tourniquet?
Say: ANZCOR (the joint Australia-NZ guideline) suggests a tourniquet for life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that pressure does not stop, if you are trained and have one.
Quote: "Arterial tourniquets should only be used for life-threatening bleeding from a limb, where the bleeding cannot be controlled by direct pressure." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "if trained in its use and one is available." (ANZCOR)
Say: St John and Healthify do not mention tourniquets. They say keep pressing.
Quote: "Keep pressure on until help arrives or the bleeding stops." (Healthify)
Say: ANZCOR treats bleeding as life-threatening if: a limb cut off above the wrist or ankle; a shark attack, propeller cut or similar major injury; pressure does not control it; or signs of shock.
Quote: "amputated or partially amputated limb above wrist or ankle" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "shark attack, propeller cuts or similar major trauma to any part of the body" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "bleeding not controlled by local pressure" (ANZCOR)
Ask: What do you have?
- A tourniquet, and I am trained -> tq-apply
- A tourniquet, but I am not trained -> tq-untrained
- No tourniquet: make one -> improvise
- No tourniquet, can't make one -> no-tq

### tq-untrained: A tourniquet, not trained
Say: ANZCOR suggests a tourniquet "if trained in its use", and says shop-made ones work best when the first aider is trained.
Quote: "we suggest to apply an arterial tourniquet above the bleeding point, if trained in its use and one is available." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Effective use of commercial tourniquets is optimal when first aid providers are trained in proper application techniques." (ANZCOR)
Say: ANZCOR does not say whether an untrained helper should use one anyway. The app can't decide this for you. The 111 call taker may help.
Ask: What will you do?
- Use it -> tq-apply
- Don't use it: other options -> no-tq

### improvise: Make a tourniquet
Say: A shop-made windlass tourniquet works better. In life-threatening bleeding, a home-made one is likely better than none.
Quote: "Commercially manufactured windlass tourniquets such as those based on military designs are more effective than improvised tourniquets." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "in the context of life-threatening bleeding, an improvised tourniquet is likely to be better than no tourniquet." (ANZCOR)
Do: Use a broad band: a triangular or elastic bandage, clothing, or a surfboard leg rope. Tighten it by twisting a rod or stick under the band.
Quote: "Tourniquets, ideally of a similar broad width to commercial types, can be improvised using materials from a first aid kit (e.g. triangular bandage, elastic bandage) from clothing, a surfboard leg rope or other available similar items." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Improvised tourniquets should be tightened by twisting a rod or stick under the improvised tourniquet band" (ANZCOR)
Say: ANZCOR suggests tourniquets "if trained in its use"; it does not say whether that also applies to a home-made one.
Quote: "if trained in its use and one is available." (ANZCOR)
Warning: A thin stretchy blood-test tourniquet will not work. A home-made one that is not tight enough can increase bleeding.
Quote: "is not suitable for use as an arterial tourniquet." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Improvised tourniquets which do not stop all circulation can increase bleeding." (ANZCOR)
Next: tq-apply

### tq-apply: Put the tourniquet on
Do: Follow the maker's instructions. No instructions: 5 cm above the bleeding point.
Quote: "in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions (or 5 cm above the bleeding point if no instructions)" (ANZCOR)
Do: If you can, not over a joint, the wound, clothing or a wetsuit. Tighten until the bleeding stops, even if it hurts.
Quote: "Ideally, a tourniquet should not be applied over a joint or wound" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Ideally the tourniquet is not applied over clothing nor wetsuits and is applied tightly, even if this causes local discomfort." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "tightened until the bleeding stops." (ANZCOR)
Warning: Tap the button the moment it is on. It records the clock time. Tell the paramedics that time.
Quote: "The time of tourniquet application must be noted and communicated to emergency/paramedic personnel." (ANZCOR)
Record: Tourniquet on
Ask: Has the bleeding stopped?
- Yes, it has stopped -> tq-on
- Still bleeding -> tq-more

### tq-more: Still bleeding with a tourniquet
Do: Check where it is and how it is put on. Tighten it.
Quote: "If a tourniquet does not stop the bleeding its position and application must be checked." (ANZCOR)
Do: Still bleeding? Put on a second tourniquet if you have one, above the first if you can.
Quote: "a second tourniquet (if available) should be applied to the limb, preferably above the first." (ANZCOR)
Record: Second tourniquet on
Say: Still bleeding after that? ANZCOR: think about a haemostatic dressing as well.
Quote: "consider using a haemostatic dressing in conjunction with the tourniquet." (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- Bleeding has stopped -> tq-on
- Still bleeding: haemostatic dressing -> dressing
- Still bleeding, no dressing: keep pressing -> care
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing

### tq-on: Tourniquet on: leave it
Warning: Do not take it off. Do not cover it with a bandage or clothing.
Quote: "the tourniquet should not be removed until the person receives specialist care." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "must not be covered up by any bandage or clothing." (ANZCOR)
Say: They need to get to hospital urgently. Tell the paramedics the clock time it went on (on the "Tourniquet on" button and in the Log).
Quote: "Once applied, the person requires urgent transfer to hospital" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "The time of tourniquet application must be noted and communicated to emergency/paramedic personnel." (ANZCOR)
Say: No source says how to write the time down (on the tourniquet or the person).
Say: ANZCOR's 2025 update: some studies found possible harm after more than 2 hours, but its advice is unchanged. It gives first aiders no step for this.
Quote: "this occurred after prolonged (>2 hours) application of arterial tourniquets." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Until the results of this review are available, ANZCOR Guideline 9.1.1 recommendations remain unchanged." (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- A cut-off body part to save -> part
- Wait for the ambulance -> care

### no-tq: No tourniquet
Say: ANZCOR: no tourniquet, or it did not work? A haemostatic dressing, if you are trained and have one.
Quote: "from a limb when a tourniquet is not available or has failed to stop the bleeding, we suggest to apply a haemostatic dressing, if trained in its use and one is available." (ANZCOR)
Do: Otherwise keep pressing hard until help arrives. No source gives another step.
Quote: "Keep pressure on until help arrives or the bleeding stops." (Healthify)
Ask: Now?
- I have a haemostatic dressing -> dressing
- Still bleeding, no dressing: keep pressing -> care
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing

### dressing: Haemostatic dressing
Say: ANZCOR only: a dressing with something in it that helps stop bleeding. Use it if you are trained and have one: where a tourniquet can't go, or when a tourniquet is not there or has not worked.
Quote: "Haemostatic dressings are impregnated with agents that help stop bleeding." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "from a wound site not suitable for tourniquet, or from a limb when a tourniquet is not available or has failed to stop the bleeding" (ANZCOR)
Do: Put it as close as you can to the bleeding point. Press it on with your hand, then bandage it in place.
Quote: "Haemostatic dressings must be applied as close as possible to the bleeding point, held against the wound using local pressure (manually initially) then held in place with the application of a bandage (if available)." (ANZCOR)
Record: Haemostatic dressing used
Warning: Leave it on until hospital care.
Quote: "Haemostatic dressings should be left on the bleeding point until definitive care is available." (ANZCOR)
Say: No dressing? Keep pressing hard. No source gives another step.
Ask: Now?
- Bleeding under control -> care
- Still bleeding, no dressing: keep pressing -> care
- Still bleeding from an arm or leg -> tourniquet
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing

### embedded: Something stuck in the wound
Warning: Do not pull it out. It may be plugging the wound.
Quote: "Do not remove the embedded object because it may be plugging the wound and restricting bleeding." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "If an object is stuck in the wound, don't remove it." (Healthify)
Do: Put padding around it, on each side. Press on the padding, not on the object.
Quote: "Apply padding around or on each side of the protruding object, with pressure over the padding." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "apply pressure around the wound but don't put pressure on the object." (Healthify)
Say: St John differs: remove a small one near the surface (a nail or staple); leave a large one in. The app follows ANZCOR.
Quote: "If it's small, like a nail or a staple, and near the surface of the skin, remove it." (St John)
Quote: "If it's large, like a screwdriver or a knife, DO NOT remove it." (St John)
Say: Pressing may not be enough. A tourniquet or haemostatic dressing may still be needed (ANZCOR).
Quote: "It may still be necessary to use other measures including an arterial tourniquet or haemostatic dressings." (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- Still bleeding from an arm or leg -> tourniquet
- Still bleeding from somewhere else -> dressing
- Still bleeding, no dressing: keep pressing -> care
- Bleeding under control -> care
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing

### amputation: A body part cut off
Do: Call 111. Press a bulky pad or clean cloth firmly on the bleeding area.
Quote: "call 111 immediately for an ambulance." (Healthify)
Quote: "Use a bulky pad or clean cloth and apply it firmly to the bleeding area" (Healthify)
Record: 111 called
Say: ANZCOR: an arm or leg cut off above the wrist or ankle (even partly) is life-threatening bleeding.
Quote: "amputated or partially amputated limb above wrist or ankle" (ANZCOR)
Say: Raise the injured part? St John and Healthify: yes, if you can. ANZCOR: no evidence it helps; it may cause more pain or injury. The app follows ANZCOR: no raising step.
Quote: "Keep the injured area raised (if you can)." (St John)
Quote: "raise the injured area if possible." (Healthify)
Quote: "There is no evidence that elevating a bleeding part will help control bleeding and there is the potential to cause more pain or injury." (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- Still bleeding from an arm or leg -> tourniquet
- Bleeding controlled: save the part -> part
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing

### part: Save the cut-off part
Do: Put the part in a plastic bag; keep it dry. Seal the bag. Put the bag in water with ice.
Quote: "Put the body part in a sealed plastic bag – keep it dry." (St John)
Quote: "Place the sealed plastic bag in a container of water and ice." (St John)
Say: Healthify adds: leave a little air in the bag, and use cold water with several ice cubes. ANZCOR says nothing about the part.
Quote: "seal the bag closed with a little air inside to protect it with a 'cushion' of air." (Healthify)
Quote: "Place the bag in a bucket of cold water with several ice cubes in the water." (Healthify)
Do: Make sure the part goes to hospital with the person.
Quote: "Make sure the body part goes to the hospital with the person." (St John)
Next: care

### care: While you wait for the ambulance
Do: Keep pressing until help arrives or the bleeding stops. No source gives a number of minutes.
Quote: "Keep pressure on until help arrives or the bleeding stops." (Healthify)
Do: Keep them lying down, still and warm. Reassure them.
Quote: "Assist the person into a position of comfort, preferably lying down" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Advise the person to lie down and remain still" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Keep the person warm" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Reassure the person" (ANZCOR)
Warning: Nothing to eat or drink, not even medicines.
Quote: "Do not give any food or drink orally, including medications" (ANZCOR)
Do: Check their breathing and how they are often. ANZCOR gives no time.
Quote: "Monitor the vital signs at frequent intervals" (ANZCOR)
Say: Arm or leg: ANZCOR says keep it still. Raise it? St John says yes; ANZCOR says there is no evidence it helps and it may cause more pain or injury. The app follows ANZCOR.
Quote: "Restrict movement by immobilising a bleeding limb" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "If the wound is on the person's arm or leg, raise it up (to reduce blood flow to the injured area)." (St John)
Quote: "There is no evidence that elevating a bleeding part will help control bleeding and there is the potential to cause more pain or injury." (ANZCOR)
Ask: How are they?
- Bleeding again or soaking through -> soaking
- Pale, sweaty, confused (shock?) -> shock
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- A cut-off body part to save -> part
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### shock: Signs of shock
Say: ANZCOR: pale and sweaty with a pulse over 100, or capillary refill over 2 seconds, and/or less awake.
Quote: "pale and sweaty plus pulse rate >100, or capillary refill > 2 sec and/or decreased level of consciousness" (ANZCOR)
Say: Healthify: pale, clammy, sweaty, fast breathing, confused, fainting, or not conscious.
Quote: "unusual paleness or loss of colour, clammy skin, sweating, fast breathing, confusion, fainting or loss of consciousness." (Healthify)
Do: Call 111 if nobody has. Keep them lying down and warm. Nothing to eat or drink.
Quote: "the person shows signs of shock." (Healthify)
Quote: "Help the person lie down if possible and keep them warm." (Healthify)
Record: 111 called
Say: ANZCOR says "treat shock" but gives no steps here. Check them often (no time given). Oxygen only if you have it and are trained.
Quote: "Treat shock" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Monitor the vital signs at frequent intervals" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Administer oxygen if available and trained to do so" (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- Still bleeding or soaking through -> soaking
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### not-breathing: Not breathing normally
Warning: Bleeding badly? Stopping the bleeding comes first, before airway and breathing (ANZCOR).
Quote: "In life-threatening bleeding, control of bleeding takes priority over airway and breathing interventions." (ANZCOR)
Do: Call 111 if nobody has. Not responding and not breathing normally: start CPR (Healthify); follow the Basic Life Support flowchart (ANZCOR).
Quote: "If the person isn't responding and isn't breathing normally, start CPR." (Healthify)
Quote: "If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, follow the Basic Life Support Flowchart." (ANZCOR)
Record: 111 called
Say: Bleeding that is not life-threatening: airway and CPR come first (ANZCOR).
Quote: "where control of bleeding follows establishing airway and commencing CPR if required." (ANZCOR)
Say: No source says what one helper alone should do when both are needed. These sources give no CPR steps. Follow the 111 call taker.
Ask: Now?
- Breathing again -> care
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### other: Other bleeding
Ask: What kind?
- Something stuck in the wound -> embedded
- Nosebleed -> nose
- Bleeding inside (no wound) -> internal
- Bruise or swelling on an arm or leg -> closed
- Bleeding from a head wound -> head
- Crushed under something heavy -> crush

### internal: Bleeding inside
Say: Think of it if there are signs of shock, pain or swelling, or blood coughed up, vomited, or in pee or poo (or black, tar-like poo).
Quote: "should always be suspected where there are symptoms and signs of shock." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "have pain, tenderness or swelling over the affected area" (Healthify)
Quote: "cough up blood or have blood in their vomit, urine or poo, or have black tar-like poo" (Healthify)
Say: ANZCOR adds: bleeding from the vagina or penis, and severe bleeding in pregnancy.
Quote: "vaginal bleeding or bleeding from the penis" (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Severe bleeding may also occur from complications of pregnancy." (ANZCOR)
Do: Call 111. Help them lie down; keep them warm. Loosen tight clothes. Nothing to eat or drink.
Quote: "Send for an ambulance." (ANZCOR)
Quote: "Help the person lie down and keep them warm." (Healthify)
Quote: "Loosen any tight clothes, especially around their neck or waist." (St John)
Quote: "Do not let the person eat or drink anything." (St John)
Record: 111 called
Say: Raise the legs? Healthify: slightly, unless it hurts or a leg may be injured. ANZCOR and St John do not say.
Quote: "If possible, raise their legs slightly unless this causes pain or you suspect a leg injury." (Healthify)
Ask: Now?
- Unconscious but breathing -> internal-side
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Signs of shock -> shock
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### internal-side: Unconscious, breathing
Do: Put them on their side. Stay with them until help arrives.
Quote: "If they become unconscious, put them on their side if they're breathing." (Healthify)
Quote: "Stay with them and reassure them until help arrives." (Healthify)
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### closed: Bruise on an arm or leg
Do: No bleeding outside: press on it, with a cold pack if you have one.
Quote: "If there is bruising to a limb and no external bleeding, we suggest to use pressure and a cold pack if available." (ANZCOR)
Warning: Call 111 if it is badly swollen or painful, or they show signs of shock.
Quote: "If closed bleeding in a limb is causing severe swelling or pain, or the person is showing signs of shock" (ANZCOR)
Record: 111 called
Ask: Now?
- Signs of shock -> shock
- Ambulance arrived -> handover
- It is minor: finished -> end

### head: Bleeding from a head wound
Do: Press firmly on the wound to slow the bleeding.
Quote: "If the person is bleeding from a head injury, apply direct firm pressure to the wound to stem the bleeding." (Healthify)
Warning: Skull may be broken? Healthify says do not press.
Quote: "But don't apply pressure if you think they might have a skull fracture." (Healthify)
Say: Only Healthify covers head wounds, under its minor bleeding heading.
Ask: Now?
- Skull may be broken -> head-skull
- Bleeding badly, no broken skull -> care
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### head-skull: Skull may be broken
Warning: Healthify: do not press on it.
Quote: "But don't apply pressure if you think they might have a skull fracture." (Healthify)
Do: Call 111.
Quote: "If you have a person in urgent need of medical attention, call 111 now." (St John)
Record: 111 called
Say: No source says how to slow the bleeding instead. Follow the 111 call taker.
Ask: Now?
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### crush: Crushed under something heavy
Do: Call 111. If you can, move the heavy weight off them.
Quote: "If possible, move the heavy weight off the person." (St John)
Record: 111 called
Do: Press a clean cloth or pad firmly on any bleeding. Support the injured part with blankets or clothes.
Quote: "Stop any bleeding by pressing a clean cloth or pad firmly on the area." (St John)
Quote: "support the injured area with blankets or clothes." (St John)
Do: Watch them closely for any change.
Quote: "Watch the person closely for any change in their condition." (St John)
Say: Only St John covers crush injuries.
Ask: Now?
- Still bleeding badly -> soaking
- Not breathing normally -> not-breathing
- Ambulance arrived -> handover

### nose: Nosebleed: pinch and lean forward
Remind: after 20 minutes: Nosebleed 20 minutes: still bleeding? Get help
Quote: "If bleeding continues for more than 20 minutes seek medical assistance." (ANZCOR)
Do: Sit them down, leaning forward. Pinch the soft part of the nose, both sides, below the bony bridge.
Quote: "The person should sit down and lean forward." (St John)
Quote: "Pressure must be applied equally to both sides of the nose, over the soft part below the bony bridge" (ANZCOR)
Do: Breathe through the mouth. Spit out blood; don't swallow it.
Quote: "breathe through their mouth while pinching their nose (the soft part)" (St John)
Quote: "Encourage the person to spit out blood rather than swallow it" (ANZCOR)
Record: Pinching started
Warning: After a head injury (such as a broken skull)? Call 111 (St John).
Quote: "If bleeding from the nose is due to a head injury e.g., a fractured skull" (St John)
Timer: 10 minutes -> nose-10
Quote: "The person should remain seated at total rest for at least 10 minutes." (ANZCOR)
Ask: Now?
- It has stopped -> nose-stopped
- A child: something stuck in the nose -> nose-object
- After a head injury: call 111 -> nose-help

### nose-10: Nosebleed: 10 minutes
Say: Keep pinching and resting, seated. How long differs:
- ANZCOR: at least 10 minutes.
Quote: "The person should remain seated at total rest for at least 10 minutes." (ANZCOR)
- ANZCOR: at least 20 minutes on a hot day or after exercise.
Quote: "On a hot day or after exercise, it might be necessary to maintain pressure for at least 20 minutes." (ANZCOR)
- St John: pinch for 10 to 20 minutes.
Quote: "for 10 to 20 minutes." (St John)
Say: The 20-minute bar counts from opening the nosebleed screen.
Ask: Now?
- It has stopped -> nose-stopped
- Still bleeding after 20 minutes -> nose-help
- After a head injury: call 111 -> nose-help
- A child: something in the nose -> nose-object

### nose-help: Nosebleed: get help
Stop: Nosebleed
Say: Still bleeding after 20 minutes, or after a head injury: St John says call 111. ANZCOR says get medical help after 20 minutes.
Quote: "if it doesn't stop after 20 minutes then call 111 for an ambulance" (St John)
Quote: "If bleeding continues for more than 20 minutes seek medical assistance." (ANZCOR)
Record: 111 called
Do: Keep pinching and leaning forward while you wait.
Quote: "The person should lean with the head forward to avoid blood flowing down the throat." (ANZCOR)
Say: Tell the helpers when pinching started (on its button and in the Log).
End:

### nose-object: Child: something in the nose
Warning: Something stuck in a child's nose (like a bead or coin)? Do not remove it. It may cause more damage.
Quote: "If the person is a child, check if anything is stuck in their nose e.g., a bead or coin." (St John)
Quote: "If there is, DO NOT remove it (that may cause further damage)." (St John)
Say: St John gives no next step. In urgent need of medical help, call 111 now.
Quote: "If you have a person in urgent need of medical attention, call 111 now." (St John)
Record: 111 called
Ask: Now?
- Back to pinching -> nose-10
- It has stopped -> nose-stopped

### nose-stopped: Nosebleed stopped
Stop: all
Do: Do not blow the nose for a few hours.
Quote: "When the bleeding has stopped the person should not blow their nose for a few hours." (St John)
End:

### handover: Hand over to the ambulance
Stop: all
Warning: Tourniquet on? Tell them the clock time it went on. The "Tourniquet on" button recorded it; it is also in the Log.
Quote: "The time of tourniquet application must be noted and communicated to emergency/paramedic personnel." (ANZCOR)
Say: The Log also has when 111 was called, pressure started, and any second pad, second tourniquet or haemostatic dressing.
Do: A cut-off body part goes to hospital with them: in a sealed bag, kept dry, in water with ice.
Quote: "Make sure the body part goes to the hospital with the person." (St John)
Quote: "Put the body part in a sealed plastic bag – keep it dry." (St John)
Quote: "Place the sealed plastic bag in a container of water and ice." (St John)
Say: Only the tourniquet time is asked for by a source; the rest of this list is the app's.
End:

## Notes
Source copy: web-fetch only (every site blocked download; see meta.json), so a quote not found may be the copy's fault. Lines in [square brackets] in source.txt are notes, never quoted. ANZCOR's 1.6 flow chart is an image and could not be captured, so the app's order (press and call, then second pad, then tourniquet for a limb or haemostatic dressing elsewhere) comes from ANZCOR's text (1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.5), not the chart. Healthify's nosebleed steps were not captured.

Which source leads: ANZCOR 9.1.1 is the joint Australia-NZ guideline and the only one covering tourniquets and haemostatic dressings, so the app follows it first where they differ, and shows St John and Healthify alongside (trap 5). Differences kept visible: (1) Raising the limb: St John (severe and minor bleeding, amputation) and Healthify (amputation; legs slightly for internal bleeding) say raise it; ANZCOR says no evidence it helps and it may cause more pain or injury, and says immobilise the limb. Shown on care and amputation; the app gives no raise step for external bleeding. (2) Soaking through: ANZCOR second pad and tighter bandage, check position, maybe remove pads, 1 to 2 pads beat layering; Healthify add more padding, don't keep lifting; St John keep pressing until the ambulance. All on soaking. (3) Embedded objects: ANZCOR and Healthify do not remove (no size exception); St John removes small ones near the surface. The warning (start and embedded) follows ANZCOR; St John's size rule is shown as a difference. (4) Amputated part: St John dry, sealed bag in water and ice; Healthify a little air, cold water with several ice cubes; ANZCOR no care of the part. (5) Nosebleed time: ANZCOR at least 10 minutes (20 on a hot day or after exercise), medical help after 20; St John 10 to 20 minutes, call 111 after 20 or after a head injury. (6) Shock: ANZCOR signs and "treat shock" by reference only (9.2.3 has no steps); Healthify signs and call 111; St John does not use the word. (7) Food and drink: ANZCOR and Healthify for severe bleeding; St John only for internal bleeding. (8) Head wound: only Healthify, under its minor bleeding heading (press, but not if a skull fracture is possible); "Skull may be broken" goes to its own screen (call 111, don't press; no source says what to do instead), never to the keep-pressing screen. (9) Not breathing: ANZCOR puts control of life-threatening bleeding before airway and breathing, but also says follow Basic Life Support if unresponsive and not breathing normally; Healthify says start CPR. The screen shows ANZCOR's priority first, then both CPR lines, and says no source tells a lone helper what to do when both are needed. (10) Training: ANZCOR suggests a tourniquet "if trained in its use" (1.1) and calls training "optimal" (1.4), but does not say an untrained helper with a tourniquet should not use one, nor whether training applies to a home-made one. "A tourniquet, but I am not trained" shows both lines and leaves the choice to the helper (trap 8); the improvise screen says the same.

Timing and records: no source gives a number of minutes to press (only until it stops or help arrives); said on care, no timer. Record buttons show the clock time of day, and so does the Log. Record buttons: Pressure started, 111 called (on every screen where calling is the step), Second pad added, Tourniquet on (the key one; ANZCOR says the time must be noted and told to paramedics, so tq-apply, tq-on and handover say the button records the clock time to tell them), Second tourniquet on, Haemostatic dressing used, Pinching started (nose). "Still bleeding, no dressing: keep pressing" (on no-tq, dressing, tq-more, embedded) goes to care, so the Log never says the bleeding was controlled when it was not. The cut-off part screen is reachable from amputation, tq-on and care, and handover repeats sealed bag, kept dry, water and ice. The nosebleed has a 10-minute Timer (ANZCOR at least 10; St John 10 to 20) and a once-only 20-minute bar (both sources), counted from opening the nose screen (said on nose-10); "Stop: Nosebleed" on nose-help and "Stop: all" on nose-stopped end it; the nosebleed route ends on its own screens, never on the ambulance handover. The 2-hour tourniquet line is shown only as ANZCOR words it (possible harm after more than 2 hours; advice unchanged); there is no reminder, because ANZCOR gives first aiders no action for it and a bar would suggest one. ANZCOR's "monitor the vital signs at frequent intervals" has no interval, so no reminder.
