Built:
- App 18: Person overboard from a boat (NZ, small boats). Main source Coastguard NZ "What to do in an emergency",
  with Maritime NZ (Mayday call, cold water), Greater Wellington Skippers Cockpit Guide (2023), Northland Regional
  Council (distress signals), Water Safety NZ (cold water) and the Tasman harbourmaster (reporting). Australian
  (Safe Transport Victoria) and UK (RYA) lines are used only where NZ says nothing, and are labelled on screen.
  - First screens in Coastguard's order: shout, throw something that floats, watch and point, stop and turn back
    (propeller warning), mark the position (a box for the GPS reading or bearing).
  - "Can you see them?" Losing sight still goes through stop and mark before the call for help.
  - Call for help: lifejacket and beacon first, then VHF Channel 16 (the whole Mayday script on one screen, with a
    box for the position you read out), 111 (ask for Police), or other distress signals. "Not calling yet: go
    back to them" is always offered, so the call never blocks getting back to the person.
  - Recovery (boat hook; UK and Australian approach, stern, ladder, heaving line), warming the person afterwards,
    cancelling the call, reporting, and a handover screen.
  - 15 Record buttons (went overboard, flotation thrown, lost sight, position marked, beacon, Mayday, back on
    board, reported...) and 5 typing boxes, all kept in the Log with clock times.
- Reviewer found 2 missed instructions (Northland's "only to indicate distress"; Coastguard's "another local VHF
  channel you know is being monitored") and 14 findings, the main ones: losing sight skipped "stop and mark";
  no way to call for help once the person was back on board; the Mayday blocked going back; script words the
  author made up. All fixed in one round.

Built:
- App 19: Opioid overdose (NZ). Main source Healthify "Naloxone for opioid overdose" (Health NZ pharmacist
  reviewed, March 2026), with the NZ naloxone kit pamphlet (April 2026), Healthify "Opioid overdose" and The
  Level. Australian lines (NSW Health, health.gov.au) only where NZ says nothing, labelled on screen.
  - First screen: the signs, "don't assume they are asleep", call 111 and say you have naloxone. Then: kit, no
    kit, or not sure (not sure says use it anyway; it won't cause harm).
  - Kit route: safe place and recovery position, get the naloxone ready, inject, then a 3-minute wait timer and
    "breathing well, or waking?". Every dose is a Record tap, so the Log keeps the count and the clock time of
    each dose for paramedics. A one-off bar about 20 minutes after the first dose: naloxone may wear off.
  - Rescue breathing "2 breaths every 5 seconds" as quoted; still not breathing leads to another dose.
  - Woke up: stay with them, keep the ambulance coming, watch for it wearing off. Handover screen.
- Reviewer found 3 missed instructions (see the kit leaflet, stay with them, they may need medical help after
  naloxone) and 15 findings, the main ones: the wait timer was 2 minutes (now 3, the only time all three NZ
  sources allow); the 20-minute bar started before any dose; no "still not breathing" answer. All fixed.
- Scores: to fix 0, to check 0, all apps. 118 of 118 quotes exact (web copy). Tests: 38 of 38 pass.
