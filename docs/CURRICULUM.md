# Guideline queue

## What makes a good guideline for this project
The app's strengths are showing only the step that matters now, keeping time, and writing down what was done and
when without anyone having to stop and take notes. So pick guidelines where those matter. Ask three questions:

1. **Time pressure.** Is it used while something is happening, with hands and attention busy, so nobody can read
   pages of text? (Minutes to hours, sometimes days in stages.)
2. **The next step depends on what has happened.** Time passed, repeated checks, doses or amounts by size, age or
   readings, branches that change as things change.
3. **The record matters but is hard to keep.** Times and decisions must be handed over, reported or justified
   later (to a paramedic, a doctor, a regulator, police, an insurer, an auditor), and there is no free hand to
   write them down. The app's Log, Record buttons and timers do this.

A guideline goes on the queue only if it meets question 1 and at least one of 2 and 3. Best are those that meet
all three. Medical emergencies fit naturally. Outside medicine the same pattern shows up in:
- **Emergencies at work:** serious injuries or chemical spills that must be reported, with the scene preserved and notice given within set times.
- **Safety and security:** bomb threats by phone, school lockdowns, missing children.
- **Outdoors and water:** man overboard, avalanche burial.
- **Food safety:** cooling and power cuts, where hours and temperatures decide what is safe.
- **Digital incidents:** a cyber attack or privacy breach, where the timeline and each decision must be recorded.

Long processes with no time pressure (leave, resigning, study admin) don't fit, even though their rules are
spread over long pages. The apps already built for them stay, but no more are added.

Use official New Zealand sources where they exist (then Australian and NZ joint bodies such as ANZCOR or ASCIA).
Level 1: a straight line with timers. Level 2: branches and repeating cycles. Level 3: inputs and look-up tables,
or stages over days. Level 4: scores, several clocks at once, parallel tracks. Level 5: the final exam.

One (sometimes two) per run, in order, unless the owner asks for something else. Mark the status when done; if a
guideline can't be found or used, mark it "skipped: reason" and move on. New rows must pass the three questions
above; say in the row which of them it meets (T = time pressure, N = next step depends, R = record matters).

Set aside (they fail the three questions; build only if the owner asks): raising a problem at work and taking on
a new employee (no time pressure). Built before this rule and kept: assignment submission and extension,
resigning, annual holidays, sick leave, tsunami and flat tyre (the last two have time pressure but little to
record).

## The final exam (last row)
Only when every other row is done (built or skipped). The builder makes the cardiac arrest app from the guideline alone,
exactly like the others. Then, and only then, a separate agent reads (never writes) the public Pocket_Resus
repository, replays its recorded cases where they can be mapped onto the new app, and reports how many steps
agree, plus every difference in plain English. Differences are expected: Pocket Resus also contains design
decisions that are not in the guideline.
